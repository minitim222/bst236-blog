MLExpert: Towards a Generalist Agentic Model for Autonomous Machine Learning Engineering

Shangheng Du, Jinxin Shi, Shuaiyu Zhang, Yiming Wu, Junpeng Zhao, Shiyang Feng, Xin Li, Liang He, Xiangchao Yan, Bo Zhang • 2026-10-15

Large language models (LLMs) are expanding from assisting scientific research and engineering to autonomously developing and improving AI systems, advancing AI for AI (AI4AI) as a pathway toward recursive self-improvement (RSI). Workflow-driven machine learning engineering (MLE) systems provide a practical path toward this pursuit through complex orchestration that manages long-horizon experimentation, while recent MLE systems adopt model-directed interaction, allowing models to autonomously organize experiments and adapt optimization strategies based on execution feedback. However, feedback from each experiment reflects several preceding decisions, and a long MLE run contains many such costly experiments. Training must therefore select useful supervision from long interaction trajectories and learn from intermediate outcomes. We propose a post-training method for autonomous long-horizon MLE optimization and apply it to train MLExpert, a 35B Mixture-of-Experts agentic model. We design the Kaggle-MLE Factory to construct executable tasks and an interaction harness for model-directed experimentation. Hierarchical agentic SFT transfers general tool-use and coding capabilities to MLE through outcome-guided supervision. Windowed Interaction RL (WIRL) treats the multi-turn decisions between successive experiment results as an execution window and assigns it a shared outcome-based reward. Asynchronous collection and policy correction support policy updates before the trajectory ends. On MLE-Bench Lite, MLExpert improves the average medal rate of the base model from 37.9% to 72.7%, achieving performance comparable to larger frontier models such as Kimi-K3 and GLM-5.3. Further experiments show that MLExpert improves performance on NatureBench Lite, agent scaffold optimization, and algorithm optimization, suggesting that the learned optimization capability generalizes to other tasks.

Designing Transformers for Continuous Diffusion Language Models

Weijian Luo, Yujian Chen, Hao Jiang, Guang Cheng, Tianyang Hu • 2026-10-15

Continuous diffusion language models (CDLMs) offer an alternative approach to parallel language generation; however, most existing studies directly use transformer architectures from either causal large language models (LLMs) or Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) for image generation, which were not optimized for CDLMs. In this paper, we conduct a systematic study on Transformer architectures for CDLMs and introduce Language Diffusion Transformer (LangDiT), combining feature and query-key normalization, wider noise conditioning, likelihood-shortcut gradient routing and calibration, and confidence-gated training self-conditioning. LangDiT significantly reduces generation perplexity in matched LangFlow comparisons, establishing a new state of the art in continuous language generation across different model formulations. Using LangFlow as a representative CDLM, LangDiT as a plug-and-play backbone leads to a new state-of-the-art model with a generation perplexity (GenPPL) of 21.72 using 1024 NFEs and 44.72 GenPPL using 128 NFEs on the unconditional generation of 1024-token sequences on the OpenWebText dataset. When adapted to Plaid-style CDLM formulations, LangDiT results in a new leading GenPPL of 11.33 using 1024 denoising NFEs, and 27.51 GenPPL using 128 denoising NFEs. These gains are achieved while retaining each model's underlying representation, training objective, and inference sampler, isolating transformer architecture as a fundamental source of improvement. Our study identifies architectural design as an important source of improvement in continuous diffusion language models.

Our understanding of complex organic chemistry during the formation of stars and planets is shaped by a handful of cherry-picked sources with bright molecular emission lines. This observational bias impedes our assessment of how universal chemical complexity is during star formation. The lack of systematic wideband spectral surveys for statistically significant samples of protostars and cores is a major roadblock to properly probing how COM inventories and abundances depend on the vast range of physical conditions, local cloud and global galactic environments, and evolutionary timescales of low- and high-mass star formation. With robust chemical inventories towards large source sample sizes, we will be able to probe for dependencies of chemical complexity on mass regime, feedback processes in a cluster, Galactic position, metallicity, and time-variable accretion. Only with statistically significant samples at high sensitivity will we be able to assess the ubiquity and homogeneity of COM chemistry across the protostellar mass spectrum and natal cores. Constraining the diversity and availability of such species is critical for our understanding of the chemical reservoir available for planet formation, which eventually shapes (exo)planetary atmosphere compositions.

Causal Fingerprints Reveal Hidden Mechanisms in Partially-Observed Dynamical Systems

Zhang Jiang, Marios Andreou, Nan Chen • 2026-10-15

In partially-observed dynamical systems, distinct hidden mechanisms can produce similar observable summary statistics while explaining individual events differently. We introduce causal fingerprints: realization-dependent patterns that track how information about a hidden state is revealed through subsequent evolution. Within a prescribed dynamical model, we compare the filtering posterior, based on observations up to a given time, with the smoothing posterior, obtained after adding an expanding window of future observations. We quantify this retrospective information gain using Kullback-Leibler divergence and track its evolution as the future window of observational data expands. The fingerprint reveals both the strength and temporal reach of hidden influence, which can vary across events and their stages. Because the observed trajectory is held fixed while hidden-state inference depends on the candidate model, fingerprints reveal how competing dynamical mechanisms explain the same observations even when selected statistics agree. In linear Gaussian systems, we derive a characteristic fingerprint timescale for the temporal reach of hidden influence. Calibrated surrogates matching the observable mean, variance, and decorrelation time can still produce distinct fingerprints when feedback memory, hidden-noise memory, or hidden forcing timescales are misrepresented. Nonlinear examples reveal mechanism-dependent signatures for extreme bursts, oscillatory variability, and regime switching, including multiplicative instability versus additive forcing, deterministic nonlinear feedback versus state-dependent multiplicative noise, and intrinsic bistability versus hidden double-well forcing. Causal fingerprints therefore provide a time- and event-resolved diagnostic for asking not only whether a model reproduces observed statistics, but how its hidden dynamics explain individual events.

Subgraph Counting with Local Node Differential Privacy

Sofya Raskhodnikova, Adam Smith, Connor Wagaman, Anatoly Zavyalov • 2026-10-15

We study the problem of approximately counting subgraphs under local node differential privacy (LNDP), a recently introduced model that enforces strong privacy guarantees in analyses of distributed social networks. We design a noninteractive $(\varepsilon,0)$-LNDP algorithm that, given any public $k$-node template graph $H$, counts occurrences of $H$ in a sensitive undirected $n$-node graph with additive error $O(n^{k-\frac{1}{2}}/\varepsilon^{k-1})$, for any constant $k$. We complement this algorithm with a lower bound showing that every noninteractive $(\varepsilon,δ)$-LNDP algorithm for counting copies of any $k$-node template graph with maximum degree greater than $1$ must have error $Ω(n^{k - \frac{1}{2}})$. Thus, our algorithm achieves optimal error for counting constant-size subgraphs, up to $\mathrm{poly}(1/\varepsilon)$ factors. Our results substantially expand the types of network statistics known to be approximable under LNDP: prior work considered only functions of the degree distribution. In addition, our lower bound yields the first known $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ gap between central and local node differential privacy when accuracy is required for all input graphs. Previous separations either only involved $\mathrm{poly}(1/\varepsilon)$ factors or only applied to promise problems.

Beyond Pairwise Repulsion: Collective Diversity via Determinant Maximization in Diffusion Sampling

Kwanyoung Lee, SeungJu Cha, Yebin Ahn, Sungho Koh, Dong-Jin Kim • 2026-10-15

Strong guidance in text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models concentrates sampling toward high-density modes of the conditional distribution, collapsing independently sampled noise vectors onto nearly identical visual prototypes. This limits utility for tasks that demand distributional breadth, most critically synthetic data augmentation. Diversity, however, is a collective property of the whole batch: a set can preserve every pairwise distance yet still collapse onto a low-dimensional subspace. Existing sum-of-pairwise noise-optimization and repulsion objectives respond only weakly to this higher-order failure. We propose DetNO (Determinant Noise Optimization), which formulates diverse generation as RKHS volume maximization on the initial noise, maximizing the log-determinant of an RBF Gram matrix over one-step Tweedie predictions. This set-level functional equals the squared batch volume; its repulsion is strongest when the batch is most collapsed and self-attenuates as diversity is achieved. An anchor-based fidelity term keeps the optimized noises near plausible regions, and Lagrangian relaxation gives a two-term objective with a single hyperparameter $λ$ governing the diversity-quality trade-off, without training or sampler modification. Across three backbones (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3), DetNO achieves leading intra-batch diversity in VendiScore and Mean Self-Similarity, and a downstream few-shot classification experiment confirms that broader mode coverage brings statistically significant accuracy gains for synthetic data augmentation in the low-shot regime. Fixing the pipeline and varying only the batch objective, both RBF-based aggregators outperform InfoNCE, and the log-determinant attains the best downstream accuracy on both benchmarks. Its diversity advantage over InfoNCE widens as the batch grows. Code is available at https://github.com/mobled37/DetNO.

WaveLLM: Wavelet-Domain LLM Entropy Modeling for Lossless Image Compression

Yue Yu, Bowei Kang, Changsheng Gao, Dong Liu, Xiaoyan Sun • 2026-10-15

Lossless image compression fundamentally relies on accurate entropy modeling, driving a recent shift toward large language model (LLM)-based methods. However, existing approaches mainly emphasize the modeling capacity of LLMs while directly serializing images into pixel or residual sequences, leaving heterogeneous signal patterns entangled within a single prediction stream. In this paper, we argue that effective entropy modeling depends not only on model capacity, but also on the predictability of the input representation. Motivated by this insight, we propose WaveLLM, a wavelet-domain LLM-based entropy model for lossless image compression. By decomposing an image into multi-level wavelet subbands, WaveLLM converts a heterogeneous prediction problem into a set of more structured and statistically homogeneous subproblems that are more amenable to autoregressive probability estimation. To bridge numerical coding symbols with the LLM's native text token space, we introduce a dedicated numerical interface featuring value embeddings and a coefficient prediction head. Furthermore, a task-adaptive prompting strategy is designed to enable a single shared model to adapt to diverse subbands within a unified framework. Built on Llama-3.2-3B with LoRA adaptation, WaveLLM achieves 2.51, 2.24, and 2.27 bpsp on Kodak, CLIC.pro, and DIV2K, respectively, outperforming the PixelCNN-based codec using the same wavelet transform by 0.43, 0.36, and 0.46 bpsp, and surpassing recent LLM-based lossless compressors on all three benchmarks.

ALMA2040: Mapping the Spatially Resolved Dust and Star Formation Physics Driving High-z Galaxy Assembly

Daizhong Liu, Gergö Popping, Pierre Cox, Andreas Faisst, Mamta Pandey-Pommier, Dieu Nguyen, Román Fernandez Aranda, Elisabete da Cunha, Andrea Lapi, Tanio Díaz Santos, Ryota Ikeda, Jacqueline Hodge, Isabella Lamperti, Manuel Aravena, Irene Shivaei, Adarsh Kumar, Roberto Decarli, Mahmoud Hamed, Umberto Maio, Gareth Jones, Jianhang Chen, Sylvia Adscheid • 2026-10-15

Interstellar dust reprocesses approximately half of the starlight emitted across cosmic history. However, dust temperature, which is essential to convert continuum flux into dust mass, molecular gas mass, and obscured star-formation rates, remains largely unconstrained on sub-galactic scales at high redshift. While single-band ALMA imaging has characterized continuum sizes for hundreds of z>1 galaxies, single-frequency measurements cannot separate the dust mass column from the local dust heating. To date, spatially resolved (<0.5-arcsec) multi-band dust temperatures exist in the literature for only ~10 extreme systems (quasars, hyperluminous starbursts, or strongly lensed galaxies) at z>1, with ongoing programs within a few years adding at most a few dozen submillimeter or UV-bright galaxies. This still leaves typical main-sequence disks largely unexplored across cosmic history. With an anticipated ~30 times improvement in continuum sensitivity (compared to the current ALMA), simultaneous multi-band receivers, and (sub-)kpc angular resolution, ALMA2040 will transform resolved dust thermometry into a systematic survey science. Here, we present the scientific rationale and technical requirements, highlighting a future statistically meaningful survey with > 100 representative main-sequence galaxies across cosmic epochs. Realistic visibility simulations demonstrate that ALMA2040 measures radial temperature gradients with ~0.5-4 K precision (L_IR~10^{12} L_sun in 2 h on-source at z~2). These measurements will break the degeneracy between intrinsic compactness and temperature gradients, deliver the first extinction-free, temperature-calibrated resolved star-formation relations, and uniquely trace radiative feedback and disk maturation from cosmic noon to cosmic dawn.

Generalization Guarantees of Randomized Block Coordinate Descent for Nonconvex Learning

Ruixi Hu, Liang Wu • 2026-10-15

Randomized block coordinate descent (RBCD) is a popular optimization algorithm for many large-scale machine learning problems. The existing theoretical analysis of RBCD mainly focuses on its convergence behavior from an optimization perspective, while its generalization behavior remains less understood for nonconvex problems. In this paper, we establish high-probability generalization guarantees for RBCD with nonconvex objectives. In the general nonconvex setting, we derive bounds on the gradients of both empirical and population risks, requiring only standard smoothness assumptions. With polynomially decaying step sizes, we tune the iteration number to balance optimization and generalization, thereby quantifying how early stopping leads to the optimal convergence rate for the population gradients. In the gradient-dominated setting, we provide bounds in terms of functional suboptimality, which nearly match the optimistic rates in convex and low-noise settings. Finally, we develop dimension-independent bounds for loss functions of a specific structure, making the generalization guarantees applicable to high-dimensional problems.

A restart rule for genetic algorithms based on Schnabel census

Anton Eremeev, Yulia Zakharova • 2026-10-15

A new adaptive restart rule for Genetic Algorithms (GAs) is considered. The rule is based on the Schnabel Census method, originally developed for statistical estimation of a size of animal population. In this paper, the Schnabel Census method is applied as a heuristic to estimate the number of different solutions that may be visited with positive probability, given the current distribution of offspring. The rule consists in restarting a GA as soon as the maximum likelihood estimate reaches the number of different solutions observed at the recent iterations. We demonstrate how the new restart rule can be applied in a GA with steady-state replacement scheme, using three different combinatorial optimization problems as examples. Computational experiments on well-known benchmarks show a statistically significant advantage of the GAs with the new restarting rule over the original versions of GAs. The new restart rule also tends to be superior to the well-known rule, which restarts an algorithm when the current iteration number is twice the number of iterations till the current best incumbent was found.

Dark Matter in the Galaxy: Insights from the Dynamics of Stellar Streams

Mohammad Ayaz, Priya Hasan • 2026-10-15

Dark matter constitutes most of the matter in the Universe, yet its fundamental nature and distribution remain poorly understood. Stellar streams, formed through the tidal disruption of globular clusters and dwarf galaxies, provide powerful tracers of the Milky Way's gravitational potential and dark matter halo. In this review, we examine how stellar stream morphology and kinematics can constrain the halo's structure, shape, and substructure. We discuss key observational signatures, including stream gaps, spurs, and perturbations, and investigate five prominent streams: Sagittarius, GD-1, Palomar 5, Orphan-Chenab, and Cetus Polar. We summarize analytical, statistical, and Bayesian methods used to model stellar streams and infer dark matter properties, highlighting challenges arising from parameter degeneracies, incomplete observations, progenitor uncertainties, and time-dependent perturbations, particularly those associated with the Large Magellanic Cloud. Finally, we discuss how upcoming astrometric and photometric surveys, together with improved dynamical modelling, may strengthen constraints on the Milky Way's dark matter distribution and help distinguish between cold, warm, and self-interacting dark matter scenarios.

Statistical Laws of Human Mobility under Large-Scale Behavioural Shock

Alberto Amaduzzi, Lucila G. Alvarez-Zuzek, Valeria d'Andrea, Simone Centellegher, Lorenzo Lucchini, Brennan Lake, Manlio De Domenico, Bruno Lepri, Marta C. Gonzalez, Armando Bazzani, Riccardo Gallotti • 2026-10-15

Human mobility follows robust statistical laws, but whether these laws persist under large, sudden behavioural shocks remains unclear. Here we analyse privacy-enhanced GPS trajectories of over one million users in California and 180,000 in Massachusetts from January to September 2020, spanning the onset and easing of COVID-19 restrictions. During restrictions, individual ranges contracted below a characteristic scale of about 5 km, while short- and medium-range trip lengths and rare long-distance trips persisted. Exploration slowed from super-diffusive to near-diffusive, and visits concentrated on a few habitual locations. Trajectory shape, once rescaled, was preserved, and the entropy reduction is largely explained by fewer visited locations rather than more regular routines. Recovery after reopening was heterogeneous rather than a uniform return to baseline, and the decline began before mandatory orders. These results suggest that restrictions reorganised, rather than simply rescaled, individual mobility, with implications for mobility-based epidemic models.

Spurious Singularities in Dipole Subtraction Cross Sections and their Damping with Convergence Factors

Michael H. Seymour, Andrzej Siódmok, James Whitehead • 2026-10-15

In the dipole subtraction algorithm, the real-virtual cancellation in NLO calculations of arbitrary observables is implemented by defining a subtraction cross section that matches all of the singularities of the real emission cross section. The converse, however, is not true: the subtraction cross section contains additional singularities, coming from the underlying Born matrix element, that the real cross section does not. We call these spurious singularities. If used as originally proposed, this is not a problem, because the value of the observable damps these singularities, but in applications such as dipole showers and matching algorithms, it can lead to poor statistical convergence, or dependence on unphysical regulators such as technical or generation cuts. In this paper, we show that additional factors, which we call convergence factors, can be applied to the subtraction cross section to damp these spurious singularities and improve the convergence. We perform the required phase-space integrals to yield modified dipole insertion operators, and verify numerically that they indeed remove the logarithmic dependence on generation cuts that arises in their absence. This paper is dedicated to the memory of Stefano Catani, with whom the work was originally started.

The Radius-Luminosity Relationship for Active Galactic Nuclei with Hierarchical Bayesian Reverberation Mapping

Hugh McDougall, Tamara M. Davis, Benjamin J. S. Pope, Paul Martini, Zhefu Yu, Chris Lidman, Geraint F. Lewis, Christian Adamcewicz, Rahma Alfarsy • 2026-10-15

Reverberation mapping of active galactic nuclei uses the time taken for variability to travel through the system as a measure of system size, with this size being used to inform estimates of the mass of the central black hole, a technique that has graduated to an industrial-scale effort in recent decades. Such large target-count, multi-year surveys have been consistently undercut by poor light curve quality and the aliasing problems of seasonal observations. These impacts compound with statistical approaches that are not consistent between works and sometimes combine inconsistent statistical frameworks. Here, we present a new end-to-end Bayesian framework for fitting lags at scale in large surveys, simultaneous with the fitting of population models such as the radius-luminosity relation, and formulated in a numerically tractable way. This procedure is a statistically and numerically robust generalisation of the stacking and quality cut procedures of past works, fully marginalising over data uncertainty and data quality. Our approach remains numerically stable when scaling to larger surveys, and can act as a basis for any Bayesian population modelling of large sets of AGN time series observations. Applied to the OzDES sample, we demonstrate that this new approach yields more than double the constraining power on population-level properties like the R-L relationship, and an order-of-magnitude increase in the number of recovered lags and masses. With this method, we go from 24 high quality lag recoveries with traditional methods to a new effective sample size of ~297, all while being robust against random chance false positive measurements.

SCCL-DT: Semantic-Conflict-Aware Contrastive Learning with Dual-Rule Teacher Distillation for Zero-Shot Chest X-Ray Diagnosis

Ting Liu, Haoran Lai, HaobinChen, Ming Liu, S Kevin Zhou • 2026-10-15

Contrastive vision-language pre-training supports zero-shot chest X-ray diagnosis, but standard objectives treat every off-diagonal image-report pair in a batch as a negative. In radiology, recurring findings across patients mean that instance mismatch need not imply semantic incompatibility, creating false-negative supervision that we term a semantic conflict. A training-trace audit shows that pair-observations selected by the online mask used during training are enriched for report-derived finding agreement, although this proxy analysis does not establish clinical equivalence. Fixed thresholds can become miscalibrated as similarity distributions evolve, while soft reweighting leaves residual repulsive gradients. We therefore propose Semantic-Conflict-Aware Contrastive Learning (SCCL), which tracks modality-specific similarity distributions with exponential-moving-average (EMA) statistics, derives adaptive thresholds, and hard-masks flagged candidates before softmax normalization. Inclusive (OR) and consensus (AND) rules define two SCCL teachers, whose bidirectional cross-modal rankings are consolidated through standard distillation into a single deployable student, SCCL-DT. Across five public chest X-ray benchmarks, SCCL-DT achieves a mean zero-shot AUC of $0.850$, compared with $0.837$ for the strongest evaluated baseline in our comparison. Code will be made publicly available soon.

Learning Changes the World and Then What Was Learned has Changed: Training and Deploying Neural Networks in the Control Loop

Vittorio Lippi • 2026-10-15

Neural networks offer critical adaptability for controlling complex systems where nominal modeling proves intractable. However, when offline-trained neural networks are deployed within closed-loop control systems, their actions fundamentally alter the state distribution upon which they were trained. This paper introduces a unified taxonomy to categorize these sequential failure modes: the initial Closed-Loop Identification Error, the deployment-induced Action Error, and the continuous Tracking Error. Through discrete-time sensitivity analysis, this work formalizes how offline-learned parameters locally guarantee a trajectory divergence upon deployment, degrading performance due to local loss geometry. To mitigate this distribution shift, we propose actionable design policies centered on time-scale separated online adaptation. These mechanisms are validated through ablation studies on a nonlinear Duffing oscillator, demonstrating that bounded continuous learning successfully mitigates trajectory divergence whereas static deployments fail, offering concrete guidelines for practitioners deploying machine learning components in safety-critical feedback systems.

Missed Detections and False Alarms in a CNN Technosignature Detector on Breakthrough Listen Data

Omer Imran • 2026-10-15

Radio technosignature searches rely on machine learning detectors to shrink the candidate pool before a human looks at it. They are validated on held-out data from the training distribution, which excludes the cases where they are most likely to fail: a technosignature has unknown morphology, and terrestrial interference is unbounded in variety. I trained a standard competition detector once, froze it, and probed it with 15,000 injections across ten morphologies and five amplitudes, placed into real Green Bank backgrounds. Two directions were tested: genuine signals with morphologies other than a straight narrowband line, and interference resembling the training data. Recall did not follow the predicted ordering, and it fell as signals brightened, even for a needle of the kind the detector was trained on (from 0.590 to 0.090). One interference morphology reached a false positive rate of 0.627, comparable to the detector's own baseline recall of 0.5525 on genuine signals. Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) showed attention along the injected path in one missed family by a pre-specified test, and visually in a second. A pre-specified, time-averaged ON-OFF filter removed false alarms and genuine detections at the same rate. The detector reads only the ON panels. Since the only evidence distinguishing a genuine candidate from terrestrial interference lies in the discarded OFF panels, it cannot tell if a signal originates from the target star; it can only tell if it resembles the training data. This explains the false alarm directly; the missed signals reflect what the detector learned to recognise, which reading the OFF panels would not by itself change.

A Bayesian Spatially Varying Coefficient Model for Surface Urban Heat Island Estimation

Marika D'Agostini, Massimo Ventrucci, Francesca K. de'Donato, Andrea Ranzi • 2026-10-15

Surface urban heat islands (SUHIs) are commonly studied using satellite-derived land surface temperature (LST), but many analyses rely on static summaries or pointwise urban-rural contrasts, providing limited inference on temporal change, spatial dependence, and uncertainty. This study models LST as a stochastic spatio-temporal process using a spatially varying coefficient formulation that decomposes LST into a baseline spatial field and a location-specific temporal trend. This structure allows persistent thermal differences and local warming rates to be estimated within a unified probabilistic framework. SUHI is derived from the posterior distribution of the baseline spatial field by contrasting the estimated LST at a given pixel with the minimum estimated LST among non-urban pixels. Posterior excursion probabilities are used to identify areas where temporal changes are supported under simultaneous probability control. The framework is applied to summer Landsat 8 LST observations for Bologna, Italy, from 2013 to 2025. Results show strong spatial heterogeneity in SUHI, with higher values over impervious surfaces and lower values over forests and water bodies. Summer LST increased overall, but local trends varied markedly across the municipality. Joint excursion sets identify areas with warming greater than the domain-wide average and smaller clusters with statistically supported cooling. These results reveal changing thermal patterns across Bologna and help distinguish areas experiencing increasing surface heat from locations where thermal conditions have improved over time. The framework therefore provides an uncertainty-aware basis for monitoring urban thermal change.

Time to Fake It: Continual Machine Learning Intensifies Unfairness in Deepfake Detection

Kajaree Das, Oliver Böse, Subarnaduti Paul, Gabriel Storch, Martin Mundt • 2026-10-15

Deepfake detectors safeguard against misinformation, rendering their failures as much a societal as a technical concern. As generators constantly evolve, detectors trained on static datasets miss newly arising threats, while progressive training interferes catastrophically. Yet, mitigating forgetfulness in terms of accuracy, the prevalent focus of continual learning (CL), is insufficient to reflect real-world impact. We therefore scrutinize the effects of CL methods with respect to evolving fairness. To this end, we analyze four dimensions of fairness across diverse demographics for detectors trained progressively on a chronological sequence of 11 real-world deepfake generation methods. Tracking fairness metrics over time, we expose non-trivial interplays between performance and fairness that depend on both the choice of CL algorithm and demographic intersections. We find accuracy degradation to typically exacerbate unfairness and, conversely, fairness improvements to remain scarce over time. Dissecting the CL method families (rehearsal, prompts, regularization), we observe that inequitable forgetting is not explained by underrepresentation alone, but likely entangled with representation learning. We thus issue a call to action for CL methods to take social outcomes into account when balancing the stability-plasticity tradeoff.

Sluggish random walks with resetting: nonequilibrium aspects

Theophilus Dwamena Frimpong, Léa Bresque, Edgar Roldán • 2026-10-15

We derive exact analytical expressions for the fixed-time probability distribution and the Laplace transform of the distribution of the first-passage time to reach a target for a sluggish random walk in one dimension subject to two different types of stochastic resetting: stochastic resetting at random Poisson-distributed times, and resetting implemented whenever a position threshold is reached. The optimal resetting rate that minimises the mean first-passage time is obtained as a function of the sluggishness parameter, while under threshold resetting the mean first-passage time to the origin is found to increase monotonically with the threshold. We also characterise the occupation-time statistics, retrieving a Lamperti-type distribution under free dynamics and under Poissonian resetting at rate for short observation times, a Gaussian distribution centred at half for long observation times, and a distinct deterministic limit under threshold resetting. To further characterise nonequilibrium aspects of resetting, we evaluate the distribution and mean of the cost incurred per reset under Poissonian resetting, and the cost rate under threshold resetting, for different (linear, power-law, exponential) cost functions, and find the same critical behaviour of the cost rate in the limits of a vanishing resetting rate and an infinite threshold, respectively. Numerical simulations validate with excellent accuracy our analytical formulae.
