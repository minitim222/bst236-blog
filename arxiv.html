XStack: A Benchmark for ML Repo Migration

Linrong Cai, Taiming Lu, Haozhe Chen, Zhuang Liu • 2026-11-05

ML code should run where researchers choose, not where it was first written. Today, machine learning code mainly lives in two ecosystems, PyTorch on NVIDIA GPUs and JAX on Google TPUs. Migrating repositories between them means building and testing across frameworks and hardware. This migration is (1) a practical need, since it unlocks different hardware for researchers, and (2) a natural benchmark: it involves different frameworks and hardware, covers whole repositories, and is long-horizon and challenging. We introduce XStack, a benchmark that evaluates agents on migrating ML repositories between these two ecosystems. Each agent receives a PyTorch repository and a clean TPU machine and must deliver a JAX repository. Its output passes if it meets our grading criteria, based on the source repository's published result, authenticated and calibrated by us. Migration by agents remains unreliable: Claude Opus 5 passes 75%, GPT-5.6-sol 45%, and every other agent at most 40%. XStack is a step toward freeing ML code from its ecosystem. We release XStack to make progress toward reliable migration across frameworks and hardware measurable.

Quantum Knowledge Distillation: State-of-the-art, Applications and Open Problems

Aldana Al-Sulaiti, Kuan-Cheng Chen, Muhammad Bilal Akram Dastagir, Ahmed Farouk, Saif Al-Kuwari • 2026-11-05

Knowledge distillation transfers predictions, representations, or other supervisory information between learning models and is increasingly investigated in quantum machine learning. Its applications extend beyond compression to cross-architecture transfer, circuit approximation, distributed learning, and classical deployment of models trained using quantum-derived supervision. This survey reviews these approaches using two complementary classification axes: teacher-student configuration and the role of quantum resources in the distillation process. Quantum-inspired methods are discussed separately from methods that simulate quantum computations classically or execute them on quantum hardware. We explain how measurement probabilities, expectation values, classical readouts, state overlaps, and circuit-level objectives support different transfer mechanisms. We then critically assess the presented results against baseline design, execution conditions, uncertainty, and resource costs. The evidence shows that distillation can improve selected quantum students, but benefits are task- and architecture-dependent, and several studies find negligible gains or even performance degradation. Evaluations on physical quantum hardware remain limited, with some testing individual quantum components rather than complete learning pipelines. Heterogeneous protocols and incomplete resource reporting prevent general conclusions about practical quantum advantage. We identify priorities for controlled benchmarking, noise-aware learning, reproducible evaluation, and efficient transfer between quantum and classical models.

ReproBridge: Building Reproducibility into ML Research Artifacts

Yubo Wang, Rui Meng, Jaehyun Nam, Tomas Pfister, Jinsung Yoon • 2026-11-05

Reproducing machine learning experiments requires reconstructing procedures from information scattered across papers, released code, and supporting artifacts. For coding agents, reproduction success can therefore depend on their ability to fill in missing details, rather than on the released artifacts alone. We ask whether preserving recovered procedures and decisions improves subsequent independent reproduction. We define a standardized artifact design comprising a refined codebase, environment setup, experiment launchers, and a reproduction guide, and introduce ReproBridge, an agent workflow for constructing these refined reproducible artifacts. ReproBridge-Bench provides 1,029 tasks from 387 experiments across 36 papers, anchored by five human-verified reference scores per task. Agents attempt tasks in fresh sessions with raw code or refined artifacts; a separate Auditor checks execution evidence. Across four evaluated models, refined artifacts raise mean reproduction success from 76.4% to 92.6% and reduce downstream API spending by 19.7% on average. The cross-model success-rate spread narrows from 21.6 to 3.9 percentage points, with all four models achieving mean success rates above 90%. Removing any component reduces success in the ablation study, with the largest losses from code and environment removal. These results suggest that preserving execution knowledge helps even models with lower raw-code success reproduce experiments more reliably and at lower API cost.

Edge-Assisted Crowdsensing for Explainable Cyclist Safety Perception

Hongsen Cao, Chia-Yen Chiang, Mona Jaber, Meihui Wang, Yasmin Fathy • 2026-11-05

Cyclist fatality rates remain high despite policy support for active travel, and autonomous vehicles add complexity to mixed traffic. Neither crash statistics nor multi-model detection pipelines scale to continuous monitoring of perceived safety along a route. We present an edge-assisted crowdsensing system: handlebar cameras supply street-level frames, a vision-language model (VLM) turns each frame into structured traffic attributes through a single prompt, and an explainable boosting machine (EBM) combines them with OpenStreetMap road infrastructure and on-bike kinematics to predict a crowdsourced 1-9 safety score. Extraction belongs at the network edge: the fastest self-hosted model needs 2.49 s per frame and scoring takes 0.4 ms on a CPU. On 1,362 annotated frames from two London routes, the EBM regressor reaches 72.2 percent binary accuracy and a mean absolute error of 1.011 under five-fold cross-validation. It is within one accuracy point of black-box ensembles. Its per-feature shape functions give exact attribution: physical lane separation is worth +0.97 rating points, a painted lane +0.70 and a construction site -0.65. Planners can therefore price an infrastructure change in rating points, and the results enter cost-benefit analysis directly. We also report inter-annotator agreement (alpha = 0.4695), an attribute audit, and a sensitivity analysis linking attribute error to accuracy. With re-encoding, a 20 s sampling interval serves 1,000 riders at 54 Mbit/s at a cost of 1.4 accuracy points, making continuous monitoring practical.

Deep-learning surrogate model for rapid quantum-noise computation in dual-pump fiber-optic phase-sensitive amplifiers

Yousra Bouasria, Debanuj Chatterjee, Abdessamad Belfakir, Debabrota Basu • 2026-11-05

Fiber-optic parametric amplifiers in a phase-sensitive amplifier (PSA) configuration are well known for their low noise properties. Quantum mechanics dictates that these amplifiers can have a 0 dB noise figure (NF) (ratio of signal-to-noise ratio at the input and output of the amplifier) under certain conditions. However, for a dual-pump configuration, when the system is highly nonlinear, fully quantum calculations of NF become non-trivial. In such cases, semi-quantum models of NF evaluation exists, however, they are often complicated and resource intensive. To simplify that, in this work, first we use a semi-quantum NF calculation framework for the generation of numerically simulated input-output dataset of NF over a large parameter spaces. Then we use the data to train an optimized deep neural network (DNN) for rapid NF prediction. Utilizing the DNN, we demonstrate about 400 times reduction in the computation time compared to the standard semi-quantum NF evaluation approach with a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.0015 dB. Using clustering we demonstrate a rapid exploration of the parameter space with respect to high gain (20 dB) and low NF (0.3 dB) targets and retrieve associated design parameters. These results illustrate the efficacy of machine learning-based approaches for rapid search of high-dimensional parameter spaces especially in compute intensive photonic systems.

Data-driven discovery of intrinsic Lie point symmetries of differential equation systems

Ilker Gurcan, Kadir Ustun, Ramazan Gokberk Cinbis • 2026-11-05

Discovering continuous symmetries directly from differential-equation data can reveal geometric structure for model reduction and scientific machine learning without symbolic determining equations or a closed-form representation of the governing system. We formulate intrinsic Lie point-symmetry discovery as an operator-theoretic problem on sampled jet-space data. Admissible generators are identified from a sampled determining operator and separated from redundant, solution-dependent, and geometrically trivial directions. The framework combines prolongation, sequential subspace restrictions, and a fixed nonlinear dictionary linear in trainable coefficients. A rank-stabilization preflight test diagnoses systems whose represented intrinsic symmetry algebra does not stabilize under dictionary refinement. On a controlled differential-equation benchmark with an analytically known 11-dimensional represented intrinsic symmetry algebra, the method recovers the complete represented generator set, reproduces its Lie-bracket structure to numerical precision, and approximates a known generator beyond the training domain. Here we show that explicit intrinsic continuous-symmetry generators can be recovered from data obtained from exact or approximate differential systems.

On the limitations of molecular representations

Acer Blake, Fergus Imrie, Charlotte M. Deane • 2026-11-05

Molecular representations such as fingerprints and learned embeddings play a central role in cheminformatics and small-molecule machine learning. They serve as the primary input for a diverse array of tasks, including clustering, dataset splitting, and virtual screening, yet their evaluation is often limited to downstream model performance on narrow benchmark tasks. We carried out a systematic investigation of the quality of molecular representations, finding that none of the state-of-the-art molecular embedding models capture graph structure, 3D shape, or ADMET endpoint similarity better than fingerprints or physicochemical descriptors. However, classical fingerprints also revealed their own material limitations. For example, we show that depending on the representation, between 225M and $\sim$4B molecules have non-unique representations in the 10.8B Enamine REAL database, with implications for virtual screening. Our findings provide an insight into the current limitations of molecular representations, outlining future research directions for the development of more robust encodings of small molecules.

Conformal Prediction for Time-Dependent PDEs

Joshua Stiller, Annika Schneider, Eyke Hüllermeier • 2026-11-05

Uncertainty quantification is crucial in scientific machine learning, where models inform safety-critical tasks such as flood forecasting, financial risk management, and thermal control in machines. Conformal prediction provides distribution-free coverage guarantees, but in time-dependent settings common to physics and engineering, these guarantees can break down, leading to systematic undercoverage. We study this problem in the context of surrogate models for time-dependent physical systems described by partial differential equations. We prove that in a function space setting, distributions at arbitrarily close times can be mutually singular, making exact coverage guarantees impossible. We further show that in discretized settings, the total variation distance between distributions often saturates exponentially fast with the grid resolution, mandating restrictions on the spatial discretization to ensure coverage. Finally, for certain settings, we show how to use principled weighted conformal prediction to obtain finite-sample coverage guarantees over growing time horizons.

Hybrid Machine Learning for Imbalanced Landing-Phase Safety Event Prediction from Flight Data

Taha Asghar, Bilal Mufti, Syed Qasim Zaheer, Luqman Ahmed • 2026-11-05

Landing-phase safety-event prediction requires the detection of rare abnormal approaches within strongly imbalanced multivariate flight data. Capturing coupled temporal behavior while distinguishing event-specific signatures across airport operating contexts remains a central modeling challenge. This study proposes an interpretable hybrid feature-fusion framework for multiclass landing-phase safety-event prediction. A long short-term memory (LSTM) autoencoder trained on nominal flight windows characterizes deviations from routine approach behavior. Its continuous reconstruction-error descriptor is fused with parameter-specific statistical flight-history features and supplied to a class-weighted XGBoost classifier to distinguish Normal, Speed High, Path High, and Flaps Late windows. The framework is evaluated using 53,750 NASA DASHlink approach windows from three arrival airports. Pooled and airport-specific learning strategies are compared alongside component-level benchmarks, repeated pipeline retraining, and feature-attribution analysis. The results demonstrate strong class-balanced detection, with mean macro recall above 0.90 across all airports under repeated partitioning and retraining. Component comparisons establish the importance of class weighting and show that continuous feature fusion outperforms reconstruction-error-only and hard-gate alternatives. Airport-level comparisons reveal benefits from local specialization at the two airports with larger datasets and from pooled training at the airport with the smallest dataset. Feature attributions associate predictions with operationally relevant vertical-path, configuration, speed, and approach-context variables. The framework provides an interpretable approach to rare-event detection and practical guidance for adapting flight-data monitoring models to airport-level data availability.

The cold substellar frontier in JWST Extragalactic surveys: a robust parallax measurement yields the most distant known ultra-cold Y dwarf

Feng-Yuan Frey Liu, Derek J. McLeod, Xueqing Chen, Ben J. Sutlieff, Ho-Hin Leung, Clémence Fontanive, James S. Dunlop, Ross J. McLure, Trent J. Dupuy, Adam C. Carnall, Thomas M. Stanton, Beth A. Biller, Isaac J. B. Holst, Callum T. Donnan, Ryan Begley • 2026-11-05

We present an analysis of JADES-GS-BD-5, an extremely cold Y dwarf identified in the JADES/GOODS-South field. Using multi-epoch JWST/NIRCam imaging spanning a $\simeq2$-yr baseline, we calibrate the JWST astrometry and obtain the first robust parallax measurement for a brown dwarf discovered in a JWST extragalactic deep field. We measure a proper motion of $μ_{\rm tot}=49.8^{+0.3}_{-0.3}$ mas yr$^{-1}$ and a parallax of $\varpi=15.7^{+1.4}_{-1.3}$ mas, corresponding to a geometric distance of $d=64^{+6}_{-5}$ pc, $\sim3\times$ further than that of the previously known most distant Y dwarfs with parallax-measured distances. We hence determine a tangential velocity of $v_{\rm tan}=15.0^{+1.4}_{-1.3}$ km s$^{-1}$, consistent with a Galactic thin-disk object. We find no evidence for binary acceleration, significant photometric variability, or a plausible host. The $J$-band spectral morphology of JADES-GS-BD-5 is consistent with existing Y-dwarf templates, while its extremely faint luminosity and overall near-infrared spectral profile confirm that it is one of the coldest dwarfs discovered to date. We therefore adopt a classification of $>$Y1, consistent with a NIRCam colour-based proxy of cY1.5. The robust parallax-determined distance allows us to determine its bolometric luminosity, $\log(L_{\rm bol}/L_{\odot})=-7.08^{+0.09}_{-0.09}$. Evolutionary modelling places JADES-GS-BD-5 in the planetary-mass regime and makes it a rare benchmark for the study of cold giant-planet atmospheres. Current atmospheric models fail to reproduce its SED in detail, pointing to the importance of disequilibrium chemistry and vertical mixing. Finally, we also report additional proper motion measurements for eight of the other nine known JADES Y-dwarf candidates, highlighting JWST extragalactic surveys as a powerful route to building a statistically useful, parallax-calibrated sample of Y dwarfs.

RM-VLM: From Natural-Language Task Descriptions to Vision-Grounded Reward Machines

Roko Parac, Christos Ziakas, Leo Ardon, Alessandra Russo • 2026-11-05

Reward machines (RMs) have been proposed as a reward formulation in reinforcement learning (RL) to capture non-Markovian tasks where credit assignment depends on history. By representing reward functions as automata, RMs expose the task's temporal structure and allow such rewards to be handled within a Markovian framework. However, they assume a labelling function, a proposition-grounding oracle for observations, which must be manually engineered for each environment, limiting their wider applicability. In this work, we address this limitation by proposing RM-VLM, a novel approach in which a large language model (LLM) generates an RM from a natural-language task description, and a vision-language model (VLM) acts as the labelling function, grounding the RM's propositions over image-based observations during RM-guided policy learning. A VLM-based value function estimator is used to assess the agent's progress towards each RM transition and derive a dense reward signal. We evaluate RM-VLM on three domains covering non-Markovian grid-world navigation, robotic manipulation, and 3D object collection. Our experimental results show that RM-VLM consistently achieves higher task success rates than existing VLM-based reward methods across all tested domains.

REACT: Towards Real-Time Completion of Skeletal Motion Signals

P. de Clermont Gallerande, Q. Avril, P. Gosselin, F. Argelaguet, L. Hoyet • 2026-11-05

Tracking user motion in interactive and immersive applications is increasingly common (e.g., video games, XR systems, smartphones). Yet, processing and transmitting human motion data remain critical challenges, as current approaches strongly rely on the quality of the tracked motion signals. In general, affordable motion-capture systems may produce unnatural animations in the presence of calibration errors, occlusions, sensor drift, high-frequency noise, or other artifacts. More specifically, we simulate occlusions and high-frequency noise and propose to mitigate their effects with REACT, an interactive online motion-completion framework operating causally, frame by frame, without look-ahead. REACT is an S4-based autoregressive model that coordinates complementary motion estimates under specific reliability-dependent constraints and exposes several interpretable internal signals. We present a system-level contribution consisting of a structured formulation of interactive motion completion together with a coherent instantiation of this formulation. We evaluate REACT against several causal baselines and architectural variants under varying levels of occlusion severity, and report their respective inference latencies. Our results show that REACT achieves a relevant compromise between reconstruction accuracy, update smoothness, and interactive inference. Finally, we characterize how its fusion behaviour changes under missing input observations.

Machine learning classification of dynamical phases in a driven nonlinear Su-Schrieffer-Heeger lattice

Chukwunaedum Patrick Izediuno, Ghada H. Alharbi, Stephan Wong, Zeeshan Ahmad, Sunae So, Minkyung Kim, Sang Soon Oh • 2026-11-05

Driven nonlinear photonic lattices can settle into steady, symmetry-broken, periodic, or chaotic states, and mapping where each occurs in parameter space is key to device design. Brute-force mapping is slow and relies on hand-built criteria, while machine-learning classifiers are usually tested on different systems, so it is unclear which suits a new lattice. We therefore apply four classifiers to the same coherently driven Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) chain of Kerr resonators: a hand-built heuristic classifier based on Lyapunov exponents and spectral diagnostics, a supervised convolutional neural network (CNN) trained on the heuristic labels, an unsupervised library-building scheme based on augmented dynamic mode decomposition (aDMD), and an unsupervised Gaussian mixture model (GMM) on four dynamical invariants that include an aDMD reconstruction error. Using a converged heuristic diagram as the reference, the CNN reaches 98.0% held-out accuracy, while the two label-free methods reach adjusted Rand indices (ARIs) of 0.916 and 0.947, respectively, without seeing any labels. We show that the aDMD library cleanly resolves the stable and bistable phases and separates symmetric from symmetry-broken periodic oscillations, which the heuristic misses, though it cannot distinguish periodic from chaotic switching. We also find that a poorly converged reference understates the label-free methods' accuracy.

Hard Issue, Weak Model, or Bad Run? Rethinking Failure Prediction for Coding Agents

Xingyue Shi, Wesley K. G. Assunção • 2026-11-05

Trajectory-based failure predictors for software engineering (SE) agents read the partial trajectory of a run to flag whether it is going right or wrong. If it is going wrong, the run can be stopped, retried, or switched to another model. We ask whether these predictors learn anything about the run itself. On public SWE-bench Verified trajectories, where each model attempts each issue once, two run-independent factors dominate predictor performance. The first is issue difficulty: a reference score that never looks at the run, only at how hard the issue is for other models, reaches 0.92 AUROC, whereas trajectory predictors reach about 0.62. The second is model ability: within an issue, knowing which model ran predicts failure better than our predictors do, and the predictors largely reproduce the overall ability ranking of the models. Beyond difficulty and ability, what predictors gain from the trajectory is statistically equivalent to zero. This confusion carries over to the actions taken after a prediction. Model switching is often motivated by the gap between the best single model's resolution rate and an any-model oracle. This gap is no larger than random variation alone produces. Per-issue switching gains nothing on held-out runs. In a repeated-run pilot, the same model often solves an issue in some runs and fails it in others. Retrying thus has real room to repair failures, but it rescues only failures caused by a bad run, not those on an issue beyond the model's ability. Current predictors cannot tell the causes apart, as they mostly predict the issue and the model, not the run. We call for failure prediction that explains why a run fails, evaluated on repeated-run benchmarks and in closed-loop, equal-budget settings.

Mitigating scanning-induced systematics in Hipparcos and implications for Gaia astrometry

Avraham Binnenfeld, Sahar Shahaf, Richard I. Anderson, Hans-Walter Rix, Shay Zucker • 2026-11-05

Space-based astrometric missions such as Hipparcos and Gaia rely on one-dimensional scanning strategies that couple the measured signals with both observation times and scanning geometry. This coupling can introduce spurious periodicities and complicate their interpretation. We present a non-parametric extension of the phase-distance correlation (PDC) periodogram that quantifies and reduces dependence on scan geometry at the period-search stage. Using Hipparcos data, we identified recurrent periodicities associated with characteristic scanning-law timescales and showed that they are strongly suppressed by the scan-angle-controlled statistic. We then applied the method to Gaia Data Release 4 (DR4) prerelease epoch astrometry, where a recurrent ~54-day feature is suppressed across multiple sources while independently known orbital signals remain detectable. The method provides a diagnostic of scan-angle dependence and a way to mitigate its impact on period searches. These results are particularly relevant for Gaia DR4, where public epoch astrometry will enable large-scale searches for companions ranging from exoplanets to dormant black holes.

The slower rotation of planet-hosting stars revisited

Avraham Binnenfeld, Amitay Sussholz, Simchon Faigler, Tsevi Mazeh • 2026-11-05

Previous studies combined rotation periods derived from Kepler photometry, stellar parameters from previous Gaia Data Releases, and planet hosts from the NASA Exoplanet Archive to conclude that statistically planet-hosting stars rotate slower than stars without detected planets. Here, we revisit these studies by using similar Kepler rotational period sample, but with an updated Gaia DR3-based stellar parameter catalogue and an updated exoplanet catalogue. We apply minimal sample cuts in evolutionary state, metallicity, and distance, and then model the stellar rotation period as a function of effective temperature alone. For each star, we derive the ratio of its observed period to the temperature-expected period, and compare the ratio distributions for the planet-host and non-host stars. For periods shorter than 20 d, the planet-host distribution of this ratio is shifted towards longer periods; the period-ratio median is ~15% larger for hosts than for non-hosts (p_KS = 2.4 x 10^-4). We also examine the recently published TESS All-Sky Rotation Survey (TARS) and find a similar trend. However, the strongly structured period distribution precludes a robust comparison between the host and the non-host stars. Therefore, the updated analysis, using more robust stellar and planetary parameters, recovers the previously reported association, strengthening the case for a genuine connection between stellar rotation and planet occurrence.

SplatAnything: Gaussian Spacing Normalization for Feed-forward 3DGS across Arbitrary Scenes

Hao Yu, Zhenwei Wang, Yongzhen Hu, Yuning Gong, Junta Wu, Xiaowei Zhou, Hujun Bao, Tengfei Wang, Chunchao Guo, Sida Peng • 2026-11-05

We identify a key limitation of existing feed-forward 3DGS methods: Gaussian attributes are typically predicted in a radial-distance-normalized space that canonicalizes a global radial statistic of the reconstructed geometry, while the spacing between neighboring Gaussian centers can still vary substantially across scene scales and camera configurations. This mismatch induces systematic shifts in Gaussian-scale targets, leading to ambiguous supervision. To address this, we propose SplatAnything with Gaussian Spacing Normalization (GSNorm), which canonicalizes a robust scene-level statistic of Gaussian spacing to provide a more consistent spatial reference for Gaussian prediction. To rigorously assess our method, we further establish a systematic benchmark spanning diverse datasets, input sparsity levels, and challenging extrapolated viewpoints. Extensive experiments demonstrate that SplatAnything achieves state-of-the-art performance, with particularly strong gains under substantial variations in scene scale and viewpoint.

Value-oriented policy distillation for interpretable wind farm power control

Max Bruninx, Timothy Verstraeten, Jan Decuyper, Ann Nowé, Jan Helsen • 2026-11-05

Wind farm power control can help the system maintain balance and avoid blackouts. In Europe, this can be achieved through implicit balancing, where a wind farm adjusts its power setpoint in anticipation of the imbalance price using information published by the Transmission System Operator. Thereby, the wind farm helps restore system balance while also reducing its own imbalance costs resulting from forecast errors. However, implicit balancing is a challenging problem since the settlement price is unknown in advance and can be impacted by the pursued control strategy. Reinforcement learning methods could help to solve this problem, but lack the interpretability needed to be deployed on real wind farms. Therefore, we propose a policy distillation framework using explainable boosting machines (EBMs), a variant of generalized additive models, to obtain a transparent control policy. To increase the value captured by the EBM model, we introduce a value-oriented perspective to policy distillation inspired by research in the field of energy forecasting. Furthermore, we suggest to include expert knowledge during model design, such that the control strategy is also justifiable to stakeholders. Using real data of the Belgian grid and an offshore wind farm, we find that our value-oriented and expert-based controller can reduce imbalance costs by 82%, while outperforming a price threshold controller as well as a policy distillation baseline using decision trees.

Finite-Time Thermodynamic Routing in an Anharmonic Collective Quantum Otto Engine

Gabriella G. Damas, G. D. de Moraes Neto • 2026-11-05

The Gibbs state determined by a thermal reservoir does not determine how a working medium approaches it or how fluctuations accumulate over repeated cycles. We exploit this kinetic freedom in an anharmonic collective quantum Otto engine. A longitudinal interaction separates neighboring Dicke transition frequencies without changing their collective matrix elements, allowing passive spectral shaping to route finite-time transport among edges with different heat costs while preserving the contact Gibbs states. Exact edge-current identities and repeated-cycle counting statistics link this routing to mean work, hot heat, and intercycle work correlations. At fixed thermal-contact exposure, structured reservoirs can increase both mean work and long-time squared precision; equal-work controls distinguish these gains from uniform relaxation-rate changes. The useful regime is mesoscopic, bounded by spectral resolution and physical contact time. An equal-cooling example and a full-spin local-noise study test the mechanism beyond ideal engine operation, the latter retaining a one-cycle precision gain despite spin-sector mixing. In a six-transmon dressed-spectrum model, passive microwave filters at fixed integrated coupling achieve equal mean work with shorter thermal contacts, corresponding to higher contact-time-normalized output, and with long-time squared precision comparable to that of the fastest sampled flat reference. The transition pathways selected by a passive reservoir thus provide a control variable for finite-time performance and accumulated work fluctuations that is independent of thermal equilibrium.

Signals, Evidence, and Context: User Scrutiny in Real-World LLM Conversations

Ahana Biswas, Yu-Ru Lin • 2026-11-05

Large language models (LLMs) often present synthesized answers without making their supporting evidence easy to inspect. We study how features of these answers relate to users' subsequent accepting, checking, and challenging in 489,283 assistant-user response pairs from ShareChat and WildChat. Broad attribution language appears in 31.9% of qualifying responses, compared with explicit URLs in 1.33%, revealing a gap between claims of grounding and preserved inspectable support. Attribution, uncertainty, and other response cues generally accompany both checking and challenging; only uncertainty shows a small statistically distinguishable pooled association favoring checking. Within Technical Help, uncertainty favors checking relative to challenging; refusals and reasoning traces favor challenging in Seeking Information. Scrutiny is concentrated in some conversations, while its apparent turn-to-turn persistence becomes much smaller when conversation-level differences and response ordering are examined separately. These results suggest that supporting verification requires attention to what evidence can be inspected, which question a user is trying to resolve, and the history of the interaction.
