Sensorimotor Alignment: A Platonic Proxy for Embodied Policy Success

Yunfei Xie, Howard H. Qian, Shiyi Lan, Kaiyu Hang, Chen Wei • 2026-10-22

Robotic foundation models are typically evaluated through costly closed-loop rollouts, which motivates cheap offline proxies for model capability. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) suggests that as representations improve, different modalities such as vision and language converge toward a shared statistical model of reality. We ask whether motion trajectories form another such modality: as embodied models become more capable, should their sensory representations converge toward the structure of physical motion? To examine this, we propose Sensorimotor Alignment ($S^2$), a training-free offline metric that measures how well a model's sensory representation aligns with the geometry of physical motion. We find that $S^2$ correlates positively with downstream robot task success across embodied model families, making it a low-cost offline proxy for prioritizing models before closed-loop evaluation. $S^2$ can also identify promising vision-language model (VLM) backbones even before costly fine-tuning into vision-language-action models (VLAs). Beyond this correlation, $S^2$ offers representation-level explanations for phenomena observed in VLAs and world action models (WAMs). With $S^2$, we extend PRH to embodied models and make sensorimotor alignment empirically measurable, offering insight into why an embodied model succeeds beyond its closed-loop success rate.

Reading, Writing, Sharing, and Interacting with the World through Video

Shengqiong Wu, Meng Luo, Bobo Li, Ziwei Liu, Shuicheng Yan, Ming-Hsuan Yang, Philip Torr, Hao Fei • 2026-10-22

Video foundation models have advanced rapidly in understanding and generating visual sequences, while video world models seek to capture the underlying dynamics of the world they depict. Despite their different objectives, both concern the relationship between video observations and an evolving world. This survey adopts world modeling as a unifying lens for examining these developments. Viewing an observed video as a partial, lossy, and temporally ordered projection of a latent temporal world, we organize the literature around four complementary operations: reading infers world properties from observed video; writing synthesizes video from specified or learned world conditions; sharing integrates understanding and generation through common modeling mechanisms; and interacting predicts and updates world states under actions and evolving observations. This perspective distinguishes temporal coherence in observed or generated sequences from consistency in the underlying world state. Across tasks, representations, models, datasets, and benchmarks, we trace a shift in emphasis from clip-level recognition and perceptual fidelity toward persistent state, controllable evolution, physical and causal validity, and evaluation grounded in interaction and downstream decisions, and we distill the open challenges that follow.

METTA: Memory-Enhanced Test-Time Adaptation for Meta-Reinforcement Learning

Ali Najar, Hosein Hasani, Amir Kooshan Fattah, Yasamin Rajabi, Mahdieh Soleymani Baghshah • 2026-10-22

Meta-reinforcement learning aims to train agents that adapt quickly to unseen tasks from limited interaction, but this requires representing task evidence over long interaction histories. Classical recurrent agents compress experience into hidden states, while transformer-based agents retrieve from past trajectories at quadratic memory and computation cost. In this work, we propose Memory-Enhanced Test-Time Adaptation (METTA) for meta-reinforcement learning. It decomposes the fast adaptation phase into two temporal scales: a causal neural memory performs fine-grained within-episode adaptation with linear sequence complexity, while compact episode-level summaries support long-range cross-episode task inference without attending over the full interaction history. Moreover, equipping agents with neural memory provides sufficient expressive capacity and test-time training capability, enabling effective adaptation to unseen held-out tasks and task families at meta-test time. On unseen held-out Meta-World ML10/45 test environments, METTA achieves a success rate over $50\%$ while maintaining a low training budget and short wall-clock time. On long-context sparse-reward PointMaze tasks, it outperforms transformer-based baselines by exhibiting more structured exploration behavior. These results show that METTA provides an efficient path toward more adaptive and scalable long-context meta-RL.

On the direct SLE-martingale approach to the integrability of Conformal Loop Ensembles

Jason Miller, Wendelin Werner • 2026-10-22

This paper is about the derivation of "integrability results" (i.e., for example, explicit formulas for the probability of events involving a few marked points) for Conformal Loop Ensembles, that are the (conjectured) scaling limits of discrete crititical planar lattice models from Statistical Physics. We discuss the explicit solutions to the PDEs provided by the original SLE/CLE martingale approach that AI now makes it possible to uncover. This provides short and direct new proofs of a number of formulas that have recently been derived based on Liouville quantum gravity, conformal field theory or Coulomb gas methods, and also makes it possible to derive some new ones. We briefly illustrate this with the examples of the backbone exponent and annulus crossing probabilities for critical percolation, but this direct approach seems to apply to a wide class of observables of two-dimensional systems related to conformal field theories.

Magnetic Activity as a Driver of Low-gravity-like Spectral Morphology in Late-M Dwarfs

Chia-Lung Lin, Daniel Apai, Benjamin V. Rackham, Prajwal Niraula, Mark S. Giampapa, Ana Glidden, Julien de Wit, Valeriy Vasilyev, Nadiia Kostogryz, Alexander Shapiro • 2026-10-22

We present a systematic investigation of the connection between magnetic activity and the low-surface-gravity-like near-infrared spectral morphology of late-M dwarfs. Our sample includes TRAPPIST-1 observed with JWST NIRISS/SOSS and 22 late-M dwarfs observed with IRTF/SpeX, together with flare observations from K2, TESS, JWST NIRISS/SOSS, and literature-reported X-ray luminosities. Using one-component PHOENIX/NewEra spectral modeling, we find that all targets are better reproduced by lower-surface-gravity model spectra than by models with the expected high gravities of field late-M dwarfs. The retrieved surface gravities moderately correlate with independently measured gravity-sensitive spectral indices, with the strongest trends seen for FeH$_z$ at 0.998~$μ$m and FeH$_J$ at 1.2~$μ$m, both showing statistically significant Pearson and Spearman correlations with $p<0.005$. They also show strong anti-correlations with flare luminosity and X-ray luminosity: for both relations, Pearson and Spearman coefficients are $<-0.9$, with $p \leq 1.0\times10^{-4}$, indicating that more active stars tend to have spectra favoring lower-gravity morphologies. Time-series spectra of TRAPPIST-1 during two large flares show no clear short-timescale variations in selected photospheric indices, suggesting that persistent small-scale magnetic activity may play a more important role than large flares in modifying the photospheric temperature--pressure structure. A simplified radiative-transfer experiment further shows that activity-associated heating can produce lower-gravity-like spectral features even when the input surface gravity is fixed. Our results suggest that underestimated surface gravities in late-M dwarfs may reflect atmospheres modified by frequent, small-scale magnetic activity, such as microflares, producing emergent spectra that mimic lower-gravity morphology.

Causal Chain of Thought: Mitigating Attention Leakage via Context Isolation

Gabriele Dominici, Teague McMillan, Fazl Barez, Marc Langheinrich, Martin Gjoreski • 2026-10-22

Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting elicits step-by-step rationales that appear to explain model predictions, but there is no guarantee these steps causally drive the final answer. In standard autoregressive generation, dense attention allows each reasoning step to attend to the full context, enabling the model to derive answers through direct lookup while generating plausible-sounding but post-hoc justifications. We introduce CausalCoT, an inference framework that enforces causal transparency by decomposing problems into a directed acyclic graph of atomic sub-questions, evaluating each node in an isolated context containing only its direct causal parents. This Markovian structure removes attention pathways to non-parent content, making downstream predictions sensitive to local reasoning rather than global context leakage. Across a diverse suite of reasoning benchmarks, CausalCoT preserves competitive predictive performance while improving explanation faithfulness, increasing causal sensitivity of intermediate reasoning steps by an average of 12 and up to 21 percentage points over the strongest baseline. The structured dependency graph additionally enables test-time interventions in which local corrections propagate predictably through downstream reasoning.

Efficient and Optimal Learning of Discrete Distributions with Person-Level Privacy

Gautam Kamath, Mahbod Majid, Ankur Moitra, Argyris Mouzakis, Jonathan Ullman • 2026-10-22

We study learning a discrete distribution under person-level differential privacy in the batched setting. Given $n$ users, each contributing $m$ i.i.d. samples from an unknown distribution $μ$ over $[k]$, we seek an estimate of $μ$ with total-variation error $α$. We give polynomial-time algorithms under both pure and approximate differential privacy. Under pure $(\varepsilon,0)$-DP, we achieve $n = \widetilde O(k/(α^2 m)+k/(α\sqrt{m}\varepsilon)+k/\varepsilon)$, the first polynomial-time pure-DP guarantee matching the information-theoretic optimum up to logarithmic factors. Under approximate $(\varepsilon,δ)$-DP, we achieve $n = \widetilde O(k/(α^2 m)+k/(α\sqrt{m}\varepsilon)+k+\log(1/δ)/\varepsilon)$. At $δ=2^{-Θ(k)}$, this is $\widetilde O(k)$ for fixed $α,\varepsilon,m$ and failure probability. Our algorithms use a new degree-6 sum-of-squares estimator for learning from untrusted batches. Its key ingredient is a trace-bounded diagonal majorizer for the difference between the empirical covariance of the repaired batch averages and the multinomial covariance predicted by their mean. We prove that clean data admit this certificate through semidefinite-programming duality and a new application of the stronger symmetric form of Grothendieck's inequality due to Friedland and Lim. The certificate preserves the mean-dependent variance of multinomial batch averages, yielding the near-optimal robust rate $O(η\sqrt{\log(1/η)/m})$ and providing the score required by the privacy reduction. We also prove a statistical query lower bound ruling out a uniformly smaller order of error under fixed polynomial SQ resource bounds.

Quantum K-structure constants and cylindric Grothendieck polynomials for Grassmannians

Vassily Gorbounov, Christian Korff, Leonardo C. Mihalcea • 2026-10-22

We use the partition functions of a five-vertex exactly solvable lattice model from statistical mechanics to give new combinatorial formulae for the Schubert structure constants of torus-equivariant quantum K-theory rings of Grassmannians. The proofs build on our earlier results where we identified the transfer matrices of these lattice models geometrically as multiplications by the Hirzebruch $λ_y$ classes of the tautological subbundle and the (dual) quotient bundle. The structure constants are solutions of a system of linear equations with coefficients given by the partition function. This system may be solved explicitly by specializing the Hirzebruch parameter $y$ to an equivariant parameter, leading to explicit formulae using Cramer's rule. Furthermore, we give an alternative combinatorial construction of the partition functions in terms of sums over cylindric set-valued tableaux, which we call cylindric Grothendieck polynomials. The latter generalise McNamara's factorial skew Grothendieck and Yeliussizov's modified Grothendieck polynomials and can be thought of as an equivariant K-theoretic version of Postnikov's toric Schur function. We also provide new polynomial representatives for equivariant K-theory classes of Richardson varieties and their variants.

Is Spectral Self-Supervised Learning a Minimax Optimal Method of Density Ratio Estimation?

Hiroki Waida, Takafumi Kanamori • 2026-10-22

Spectral self-supervised learning is a method of density ratio estimation for the setting in which only samples drawn from the joint density of the numerator are available. The method has extensively been studied in terms of the computational properties, such as optimal solutions and connections to representation learning. However, theoretical understanding of its statistical property is still insufficient. In this work, we study three questions, namely, the minimax optimality, the applicability of the theory to regularized algorithms, and the statistical merits of density ratio estimation with data augmentation mechanisms that have recently been considered in the context of self-supervised representation learning. We first show that for general classes of Hölder continuous density ratios, there are regularized spectral self-supervised learning algorithms that yield estimators attaining the minimax optimality, up to logarithmic factors. We then prove that for subclasses endowed with a data augmentation mechanism, we obtain faster convergence rates that are also minimax optimal (up to logarithmic factors) in a weaker sense. We also present the key proof ideas tailored for self-supervised density ratio estimation.

Towards Systematic Control of the Nucleon Gluon PDF with the Pseudo-PDF Method

Alex NieMiera, Kai Ebira, William Good, Huey-Wen Lin • 2026-10-22

We present updated lattice quantum chromodynamics (QCD) results for the unpolarized nucleon gluon parton distribution function (PDF) using the pseudo-PDF methodology. The calculation is performed at valence pion masses $M_π\approx 310$ and $690$ MeV on an $a \approx 0.06$ fm ensemble with $2+1+1$ flavors of highly improved staggered quarks (HISQ) generated by the MILC Collaboration. This lattice spacing is finer than those used in previous gluon pseudo-PDF studies, providing access to multiple short-distance separations and nucleon boost momenta as high as $4.4$ GeV. To control excited-state contamination and reduce model dependence, we employ the recently developed Lanczos method, finding good agreement with our previous two-state fit approach. Higher-twist contributions and discretization effects are accounted for through explicit parameterizations in fits to the renormalized short-distance matrix elements. Together, these advances represent important steps toward systematically controlled, high-precision extractions of the gluon PDF. The resulting PDFs exhibit no statistically significant dependence on the valence pion mass and indicate a strongly suppressed gluon distribution at large $x$, consistent with a subset of phenomenological PDF extractions. These results demonstrate progress toward providing systematically controlled lattice-QCD constraints for future global PDF analyses.

More efficient transportation of causal effects using mediators

Xinyi Zhang, Kara E. Rudolph, James Russell Pike, Richard Liu, Michele Santacatterina, Iván Díaz • 2026-10-22

We develop flexible semiparametric estimators of the average treatment effect transported to a target population that leverage mediators to achieve identification, and prior knowledge of population heterogeneity or summary-level statistics to achieve improved efficiency. We first propose a one-step semiparametric estimator that assumes knowledge of the baseline covariates driving the heterogeneity across populations. This approach improves efficiency by transporting only through these covariates, rather than through the full covariate set. We then introduce a collaborative one-step semiparametric estimator that also improves efficiency without requiring knowledge of which covariates drive study heterogeneity. Collectively, these estimators advance our ability to transport causal effects by improving efficiency in the presence of mediation and intermediate confounding through targeted dimension reduction strategies. We use simulation to examine finite-sample performance and apply our estimators to a large-scale housing mobility trial (MTO) and a hearing intervention trial (ACHIEVE).

A Generalized Framework for Rapid Polarization Response Generation in Wide Field-of-View Compton Polarimeters

Mohamed Gamal Zaid, Merlin Kole, Mark McConnell, Fabio Muleri, Hancheng Li • 2026-10-22

Polarimetry is the most direct probe of the unsettled radiation mechanism and magnetic field structure of gamma-ray burst (GRB) prompt emission at the energies where it peaks, tens of keV to about 1 MeV. Compton polarimeters use the azimuthal scattering angle distribution (ASAD), a 180 degree modulation on axis. GRB measurements have largely relied on the on-axis modulation factor $μ_{100}$. For a wide field-of-view planar polarimeter, GRBs arrive off axis, where the Klein-Nishina projection adds harmonics to the ASAD. Muleri (2014) described this projection and evaluated it in a low-energy expansion for X-rays. We evaluate it numerically without approximation over the whole prompt-emission energy band for any incidence and polarization angles, quantify the off-axis harmonics a single modulation factor cannot capture, and verify the model against GEANT4 simulations of an ideal polarimeter. We also include the effect of the detection threshold on the ASAD. With POLAR as the case study, we present two modes that build the full polarization response with at least 95 percent fewer Monte Carlo simulations. Mode 1 takes only the unpolarized simulation per energy and recovers the polarization measured with POLAR's full GEANT4 responses for all but the brightest GRBs, where it underestimates the polarization degree. Mode 2 adds two polarized simulations per energy at distinct angles and reproduces the simulated ASADs to their statistical noise and the polarization of every GRB tested. Both modes are released as open-source software for any planar Compton polarimeter.

A novel machine learning framework based on proper orthogonal decomposition for turbine wake flow predictions in natural waterways

Mustafa Meriç Aksen, Hossein Seyedzadeh, Mehrshad Gholami Anjiraki, Ali Khosronejad • 2026-10-22

We developed a machine learning (ML) surrogate trained on instantaneous large eddy simulation (LES) data filtered using proper orthogonal decomposition (POD) to predict mean flow fields in natural rivers with complex geometries. The surrogate enables efficient prediction of mean flow fields downstream of marine hydrokinetic (MHK) turbine arrays at a fraction of the computational cost of high fidelity simulations, facilitating turbine layout optimization in natural waterways where bathymetry, turbulence, and turbine interactions influence the available energy resource. We demonstrate the capabilities of the model at a real world tidal river site under ebb tide conditions. Complex bed bathymetry and planform geometry generate substantial background turbulence that can obscure turbine wake signatures. POD is employed to extract relevant flow structures, improving the ability of the ML model to learn wake dynamics. First, LES is performed at the tidal site to generate training data. POD is then applied to decompose the instantaneous LES fields into spatial modes. Selected modes are used to train a three dimensional convolutional encoder decoder network to predict time averaged velocity fields. This selective filtering reduces the influence of dominant background fluctuations while preserving information relevant to turbine wakes. At equivalent network size and training cost, the POD filtered surrogate reduces the mean squared error by three orders of magnitude compared with the same network trained on unfiltered LES data.

SpecLess: A Multi Survey Python Package for Machine Learning Classification of Galaxy Morphology and Activity from Photometry

Uzay Aydin, Mehmet Tanriver • 2026-10-22

Broad band photometry enables scalable galaxy classification without spectroscopy at inference time, yet strong within survey performance may not transfer when the same galaxies are measured by another survey. We present SpecLess, an open source Python package implementing two photometric classification tasks: high confidence Galaxy Zoo 1 elliptical and spiral morphologies, and four SDSS spectroscopic activity reference classes (starforming, starburst, Seyfert, and LINER). Using SDSS+AllWISE, KiDS+VIKING, and DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys photometry, survey specific XGBoost classifiers achieve held out macro F1 scores of 0.9332 (SDSS; 35,554 galaxies) and 0.9237 (KiDS; 3,269) for morphology, and 0.7637 (SDSS; 16,931) and 0.7398 (DESI; 11,975) for activity. Starforming and starburst references are recovered more effectively than Seyfert and LINER, while feature ablations reveal complementary contributions from optical and mid infrared photometry. We quantify catalogue dependence using separately trained common feature models evaluated on matched galaxies with identical labels and data partitions. Relative to target trained models, macro F1 deficits are 0.0153 (SDSS to KiDS) and 0.0354 (KiDS to SDSS) for morphology (12,301 galaxies; 20 splits), and 0.0660 (SDSS to DESI) and 0.0406 (DESI to SDSS) for activity (11,975 test galaxies). Colour only refits, additive corrections, and optical infrared substitutions reveal direction dependent sensitivity without consistently recovering target trained performance. SpecLess v0.3.0 distributes four pretrained, survey specific classifiers through a unified Python interface. The package provides a reproducible baseline for photometric galaxy classification across multiple surveys while establishing an extensible, open source foundation for future developments in classification methods and photometric catalogue support.

LACToS: Local Anomaly Detection via Conditional Typicality of Superpixels

Jihoon Hong, Alireza Kheirandish, Rohan Sanda, Sara Fridovich-Keil • 2026-10-22

Local anomaly detection is the task of identifying spatially confined anomalous or out-of-distribution (OOD) regions within an otherwise in-distribution (ID) image. Most existing methods are designed for a specific context, with a strong focus on industrial defect detection, where they achieve excellent performance on well-established benchmarks. However, we observe that these methods can exhibit significant performance drops in other contexts, especially when the inlier distribution is multimodal. To account for this inlier complexity, we propose LACToS, a principled local OOD detection method based on statistical typicality and made tractable through two innovations: (i) grouping pixels into superpixels and (ii) using a computationally efficient proxy to estimate typicality. We evaluate LACToS across three diverse applications spanning medical imaging, human faces, and X-ray security screening. LACToS performs more robustly than prior methods across these benchmarks. The code is released here: https://github.com/voilalab/LACToS.

Causal inference in staggered adoption panels: The correct comparison set depends on your intervention

Ian Lundberg, Yaling Xu • 2026-10-22

A powerful data structure for causal inference is staggered adoption: many units are observed over many time periods, during which some units adopt an irreversible treatment at various time points while others remain untreated. Popular methods that apply in this setting include difference in difference, fixed effects, matching, and synthetic control. All of these methods compare the future outcomes of treatment adopters and non-adopters to answer the question: for units who adopt treatment, what outcomes would have been realized if they had remained untreated? We show that this question actually hides two very different causal questions of interest. The first is what would have happened under a momentary intervention to prevent treatment at its factual adoption time. The second is what would have happened to a treated unit under a longitudinal ban preventing treatment at all periods until outcome measurement. We show how to answer each question under conditionally ignorable treatment adoption or under parallel trends. We highlight that common methods cannot answer the second question because this question requires a time-varying comparison set with time-varying weights.

Nonparametric autoregressive estimation of probability distributions with parallel gradient boosting

Rémy Chapelle • 2026-10-22

Nonparametric estimation of joint probability distributions is a core problem in statistics, which is notoriously challenging with high-dimensional data. Such challenges also arise with conditional distribution estimation and have led to flexible algorithms that aim to tackle the ''curse of dimensionality'' by exploiting predictive structures of lower dimension than the ambient space. In this paper, we study the use of such conditional distribution estimators to estimate joint distributions by factorizing the latter in an ''autoregressive'' way. We show that for loss functions satisfying a subadditivity property, bounds on the convergence rate of an autoregressive estimator can be derived from bounds on the convergence rate of the conditional distribution estimators. These bounds relate the difficulty of estimating the joint distribution to that of estimating the most challenging factor in the autoregressive factorization. When the conditional estimators adapt to sparsity in their conditioning sets, we characterize this difficulty through variable elimination in an undirected dependence graph. This allows bounding the rates of convergence by quantities related to the sparsity of the graph, and provides an additional motivation for the autoregressive approach. We then evaluate the practical performance of an autoregressive estimator based on parallel gradient boosting, a flexible nonparametric method for estimating conditional distributions. The estimator demonstrates competitive estimation performance on various simulated and real datasets, even without optimizing the factorization order. Together, these results support nonparametric autoregressive estimation as a practical approach to flexible joint distribution estimation.

Maximum Likelihood Estimation and Likelihood Ratio Tests for the Power Spherical Distribution

Sharindi Amasha Samaraweera, Leif Ellingson • 2026-10-22

The Power Spherical distribution has been proposed as a computationally efficient alternative to the von Mises-Fisher distribution for data on the unit sphere, with reduced computational cost for simulation and a simpler normalizing constant. Despite these advantages, its statistical inference properties remain underdeveloped. In this paper, we develop a likelihood-based framework for estimation and inference for the Power Spherical distribution. Within this framework, we develop maximum likelihood estimation procedures for both the central direction and concentration parameter. Building on these estimators, we formulate likelihood ratio tests for hypotheses involving known and unknown parameters, study their asymptotic behavior, and evaluate finite-sample performance through simulation studies. The results demonstrate accurate Type I error control and good power when the asymptotic chi-square approximation is appropriate, while the parametric bootstrap provides reliable inference in small-sample settings or those where the dimension is large relative to the sample size. The findings also highlight nontrivial dependence of the power of tests for the central direction on the concentration parameter.

Benchmarking Recent Single-Cell Synthetic Data Generation Methods on the OneK1K Dataset

Buse Giledereli, Basar Temiz, Amirreza Sattarzadeh Khanehbargh • 2026-10-22

Synthetic single-cell RNA-seq data generation offers a promising route for privacy-aware biomedical data sharing, but donor-linked datasets require careful balancing of biological utility and privacy risk. In this work, we benchmark synthetic generation methods for the CAMDA 2026 ELSA Health Privacy Challenge using the donor-split OneK1K dataset, comparing scGFT, scLDM, and Cell2Sentence across statistical fidelity, downstream biological preservation, and membership inference vulnerability. Because full-transcriptome generation is computationally demanding, we focus generative modeling on highly variable genes and evaluate alternative strategies for reconstructing non-HVG expression. Our results show that non-HVG handling is a central determinant of privacy: directly copying non-HVG values leads to severe leakage, whereas Poisson-based reconstruction substantially reduces membership inference risk. We further find that scGFT and scLDM better preserve biological structure than an independent Poisson baseline, while maintaining near-random privacy profiles under appropriate configurations. These findings highlight the importance of evaluating synthetic single-cell data beyond marginal similarity alone, emphasizing robustness, interpretability, and privacy-conscious design choices throughout preprocessing, generation, and post-processing.
