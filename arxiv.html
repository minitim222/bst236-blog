OGLE-2016-BLG-0523: A Low-mass Brown Dwarf or super-Jupiter in a $>100$ au Orbit

Hongjing Yang, Weicheng Zang, Yoon-Hyun Ryu, Andrzej Udalski, Yuchen Tang, Michael D. Albrow, Sun-Ju Chung, Andrew Gould, Cheongho Han, Kyu-Ha Hwang, Youn Kil Jung, In-Gu Shin, Yossi Shvartzvald, Jennifer C. Yee, Sang-Mok Cha, Dong-Jin Kim, Hyoun-Woo Kim, Seung-Lee Kim, Chung-Uk Lee, Dong-Joo Lee, Yongseok Lee, Byeong-Gon Park, Richard W. Pogge, Przemek Mróz, Michał K. Szymański, Jan Skowron, Radoslaw Poleski, Igor Soszyński, Paweł Pietrukowicz, Szymon Kozłowski, Krzysztof A. Rybicki, Patryk Iwanek, Krzysztof Ulaczyk, Marcin Wrona, Mariusz Gromadzki, Mateusz J. Mróz, Jiyuan Zhang, Shude Mao, Hongyu Li, Qiyue Qian • 2026-10-09

To search for very wide-orbit binary-lens events, we cross-match microlensing events detected by OGLE-IV and KMTNet between 2011 and 2024. After removing contaminants from variable stars, we identify OGLE-2016-BLG-0523 as a microlensing event showing two short-lived bumps in its light curve, with the two separated by 8.36 yr. We investigate three scenarios to explain the two bumps, including a binary-lens single-source (2L1S), a single-lens binary-source (1L2S), and two unrelated single-lens single-source events (2$\times$1L1S). The 1L2S interpretation is excluded due to its poorer fit to the data, while the 2$\times$1L1S scenario is disfavored by the low event rate, unphysical total source flux, and unphysical color for one of the two putative sources. For the surviving 2L1S interpretation, the Einstein timescales of the two bumps are $8.343 \pm 0.076$ days and $3.231 \pm 0.047$ days, respectively, implying a binary mass ratio of $q = 0.15$. A Bayesian analysis indicates that the primary lens is most likely a low-mass M dwarf, while the companion is probably a low-mass brown dwarf or super-Jupiter. The inferred projected separation is about 450 au, making the companion the widest low-mass object discovered via microlensing to date. Our discovery highlights both the promise and challenges of identifying very wide, low-mass companions through microlensing. Although upcoming facilities such as Roman and Earth 2.0 space telescopes will improve sensitivity to such events, overcoming degeneracies and distinguishing them from variable phenomena will remain essential for building a robust statistical sample.

Hide and Seek: HIDE Concepts to SEEK explanations for black box models

Bhanu Tokas, Rahul Nair, Rockwell Jackson, Hannah Kerner • 2026-10-09

Interpreting machine learning model predictions is a central challenge, particularly for black-box vision models. Concept-based attribution methods interpret models using human-understandable concepts. Existing concept-based methods can only interpret classification and regression models. They require access to model weights or large concept-annotated datasets, and most methods cannot provide image-level attribution. We propose Hide and Seek (HnS), a new method for concept-level attribution that does not require access to model weights or labeled concept datasets. HnS can interpret a wide range of vision models. Given a concept and image set, HnS retrieves concept-aligned images using a vision-language model, localizes the concept within each image, masks the localized region, and measures attribution from the change in the black-box model's output between the original and masked images. This enables faithful attribution by directly measuring the model's behavior and supporting local attributions. We validated HnS on CelebA and COCO-Stuff. Compared to existing methods, HnS showed better agreement with ground-truth attributions. We demonstrated the applicability of HnS on diverse tasks, including image captioning and satellite image segmentation, where no existing concept attribution method can be used. We provided our anonymous code in the supplementary material.

Where to Look Matters: Rethinking Sub-Volume Sampling in 3D Medical Self-supervised Learning

Junkai Liu, Le Zhang • 2026-10-09

Self-supervised learning on 3D medical images commonly relies on random sub-volume sampling during pretraining. However, random cropping implicitly treats all spatial regions as equally worth observing, despite anatomical priors and spatial redundancy that make regions inherently unequal in their value for representation learning. To this end, we first show through controlled crop-quality manipulations that better sub-volumes lead to better downstream transfer, revealing the observation policy as an overlooked bottleneck in 3D medical SSL. Motivated by this finding, we revisit sub-volume sampling as a \emph{where-to-look} problem. We propose \textbf{\method}, a plug-and-play framework that replaces random cropping with an offline coarse-to-fine probe bank guided by \textit{anatomy-awareness}, \textit{informativeness}, and \textit{diversity}. We instantiate \method as a lightweight \lite variant using geometric and image-statistical cues, and a diagnostic \feat variant using frozen visual features, to examine whether lightweight volume-intrinsic priors suffice or richer semantic cues reliably provide additional value. Extensive experiments across multiple 3D medical SSL backbones and downstream tasks demonstrate that \method-\lite consistently improves transfer performance over random sampling. Mechanistic analyses further attribute these gains to more anatomically plausible and informative observations, reduced spatial redundancy, more efficient learning dynamics, and more structured representations.

Fundamental Limits of Covert queueing under Busy-Period Observations

Anumula Venkata Sai Sree Sahithi, Arti Yardi, Jithin Ravi • 2026-10-09

Consider a queueing system operated by Bob, which is intended to serve only jobs generated by a legitimate user, Willie. Willie's jobs arrive according to a Poisson process, and the service times are exponentially distributed. Bob seeks to covertly serve additional jobs arriving according to an independent Poisson process, without Willie detecting their presence. As the legitimate user, Willie is provided with certain statistics of each busy period by Bob. In particular, we consider two statistics: (i) the number of jobs served during each busy period and (ii) the duration of each busy period. We establish that, under either statistic, the maximum expected number of additional jobs that Bob can covertly serve obeys a square-root law. Specifically, over n busy periods, Bob can covertly serve additional jobs in the order of square root of n, whereas any scaling which is above square root of n enables Willie to asymptotically detect the presence of these additional jobs with vanishing error probability. Furthermore, we characterize the scaling constant associated with the square-root law, which we refer to as the covert queueing constant, for each of the two statistics. We compare the scaling constants corresponding to the two statistics and argue that the scaling constant under statistic (i) is no greater than that under statistic (ii).

Accuracy of Vibrational Properties of Machine-Learning Force Fields for Molecular Crystal Transformations

Natalia Goncharova, Oskar Schweighofer, Johannes Hoja • 2026-10-09

Machine-learning force fields (MLFFs) enable the calculation of transformation pathways in molecular crystals at a fraction of the computational cost of density functional theory (DFT). However, a good agreement in energies and structures does not necessarily imply an accurate description of vibrational properties, which are essential for characterizing transition states and determining free energies at finite temperatures. Herein, we assess the accuracy of system-specific SO3krates MLFFs trained on PBE+MBD reference data for describing vibrational properties of molecular crystal transformations. As examples, we consider a polymorph transition of 1,4-diiodobenzene and a single-crystal-to-single-crystal Diels-Alder reaction. The transition state structures obtained from solid-state nudged-elastic-band (NEB) calculations with the MLFFs exhibit a single imaginary $Γ$-point frequency associated with the respective transformation. Compared with PBE+MBD, the mean absolute errors of $Γ$-point vibrational frequencies and relative harmonic free energies at 300 K are about 3 cm$^{-1}$ and 1 kJ/mol, respectively. Furthermore, we compare for the Diels-Alder reaction a collective transformation pathway with a sequential one, in which individual diene-dienophile pairs react successively, and demonstrate that PBE0+MBD single-point corrections can significantly affect the predicted reaction energetics. These results demonstrate that system-specific MLFFs can reproduce not only the energies and forces but also the local curvature of the potential-energy surface with high accuracy, enabling reliable calculations of harmonic vibrational free energies, providing a basis for extending the studies of molecular crystal transformations to larger supercells.

Hitting-Time Bounds for Asynchronous Q-Learning

Yang Peng • 2026-10-09

We study the last iterate of standard asynchronous Q-learning from a single trajectory of a finite Markov decision process. The trajectory follows a fixed behavior policy whose state--action chain is irreducible and may be periodic. For discount factor $γ$, write $H=(1-γ)^{-1}$, and let $μ_{\min}$ be the minimum stationary state--action probability and $\mathfrak h_\star$ the largest expected hitting time of the reverse state--action chain. We prove that zero-initialized Q-learning with a constant step size achieves sup-norm error at most $\varepsilon$ with high probability using $\widetilde{O}(H^4/(μ_{\min}\varepsilon^2)+\mathfrak h_\star)$ transitions, for $0<\varepsilon\le1$. The statistical term retains the quartic effective-horizon dependence of standard Q-learning, while initialization cost is additive in $\mathfrak h_\star$. For aperiodic behavior chains, a comparison with mixing time yields a transient term $\widetilde{O}(t_{\mathrm{mix}}/μ_{\min})$. An empirical state--action count rule selects a constant step size for a subsequent training run and attains the same rate without prior knowledge of model parameters, target accuracy, or confidence level. The output is the last training iterate, with both stages included in the sample count. The proof uses two deterministic Bellman envelopes to separate statistical error from fixed-policy initialization error and adapts reverse-time hitting-cost compensation from TD learning to state--action propagation. An exploration lower bound holds for arbitrary predictable step sizes. Together with the existing statistical lower bound and its extension to pilot-selected constant steps, it yields matching mean-square rates up to logarithmic factors on the stated large-sample parameter classes.

Bicycle Race: small two-block codes on the Pareto frontier

Gideon Lee • 2026-10-09

Two-block group-algebra codes are among the simplest quantum LDPC codes, and many of them are translation invariant on a twisted torus, which suits neutral-atom arrays and other platforms with movable qubits. We ask how far they go at small sizes when searched systematically across all groups. To tackle this question, we use a series of algebraic constraints and screens to design a search pipeline capable of enumerating broad and complete swathes of two-block codes on a laptop. With it we census about $10^8$ two-block codes with up to $320$ qubits at check weights five to nine. Several of these censuses are exhaustive, and support complete statements; for instance, the Gross code is, up to equivalence, the only abelian weight-six two-block code with $n \le 200$ to reach $kd^2/n=12$, and the known $[[64,18,8]]$ is the best two-block code by $kd^2/n$ across weights five to nine with $n \le 94$. The censuses are also large enough to give insights into where good codes live - they concentrate in a handful of groups; abelian groups give the best codes at small $n$ and non-abelian groups at large $n$, with the crossover appearing to move out as the check weight grows; and high quality codes become $100$ to $1000$ times rarer per draw from weight six to weights eight and nine. Using the group algebra and the census data to steer a statistical search finds four to fifteen times as many good codes as uniform draws in equal time. Among the codes found are $[[102,20,10]]$ and $[[120,24,10]]$ at check weight nine, which we simulate in noisy syndrome-extraction circuits, and $[[288,16,16]]$, the best weight-six two-block code with $n \le 300$. At check weights seven to nine, our best codes exceed the best published $kd^2/n$ with exact distance at the same check weight in most length bands up to $320$ qubits. All codes are available in a public database.

The Redshift-Space Galaxy Power Spectrum at Two-Loop Order

Oliver H. E. Philcox • 2026-10-09

Contemporary large-scale structure analyses center around the galaxy power spectrum, modeled with one-loop perturbation theory. This description naturally breaks down at large wavenumbers, prohibiting the extraction of cosmological information from small-scale clustering. In this work, we present a complete model for the redshift-space galaxy power spectrum up to two-loop order, derived in the framework of the Effective Field Theory of Large Scale Structure. This accounts for a wide variety of effects, including fifth-order galaxy bias, redshift-space distortions, small-scale non-linearities, spatial non-locality, long-wavelength bulk flows, coordinate distortions, and stochasticity, involving a total of 49 free coefficients. Our model additionally encodes all deterministic contributions to the one-loop bispectrum and trispectrum (as well as the tree-level quadspectrum and pentaspectrum), which depend on many of the same parameters. We implement the model numerically, using the \textsc{vegas} algorithm to precompute the loop integrals; this allows the full likelihood to be evaluated in milliseconds. The approach is validated using power spectra measured from high-resolution simulations, considering both dark matter and realistic galaxy samples. The extension to two-loop order greatly increases the range of scales over which the power spectrum can be modeled, with $k_{\rm max}$ rising from $0.2h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ to $0.34h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ for a luminous red galaxy sample at $z=0.5$. Much of the additional information is absorbed by the numerous fifth-order biases and counterterms; for this reason, constraints on $σ_8$ are not significantly enhanced by the inclusion of two-loop terms. Future work will assess the full implications of the two-loop model for cosmological parameter inference, both alone and in combination with higher-order statistics.

Statistical Optical Replication and Mixing of Spatial Information across Wavelengths

Wanting Hou, Wei Liu, Hong-Chao Liu, Zhiyuan Ye, Jun Xiong • 2026-10-09

Accessing and combining the spatial information carried by light at different wavelengths is important for linking structured-light encoding with spectrally distinct detection channels. Here, we introduce a correlation-enabled linear-optical framework for cross-wavelength imaging, bidirectional replication, and spatial information mixing. Shared random holographic modulation establishes correlations between nondegenerate optical fields, allowing spatial information to be retrieved from joint intensity fluctuations. In the demonstrated replicator, a spatial pattern encoded at one wavelength is read out in the detection coordinates of another through intensity correlations. Selecting diffraction-order pairs produces correlation kernels containing either the product or the conjugate product of two input field envelopes. These operations reproduce the spatial algebra underlying sum- and difference-frequency generation, while the optical fields retain their original carrier frequencies. The theory accounts for wavelength-dependent propagation and coordinate scaling, linking the input structures to observable correlation patterns. Together, these results establish a common framework for cross-wavelength spatial information processing through engineered optical correlations and joint detection.

Revisiting the Task of Building Damage Assessment by Satellite Imaging

Anthony Cioppa, Virgile Blua, Sébastien Piérard, Pietro Ceccato, Marc Van Droogenbroeck • 2026-10-09

Natural disasters such as earthquakes, tropical storms, and floods cause extensive damage to buildings, and rapid, reliable damage maps are essential to guide emergency response. Automating this task, referred to as building damage assessment (BDA), from satellite imagery, has become an active research topic at the intersection of remote sensing, computer vision, and machine learning, particularly since supervised deep learning methods were shown to outperform earlier handcrafted approaches. Yet, despite a decade of intense activity and the release of several large-scale datasets, the field lacks a shared foundation. This chapter revisits the task of BDA from first principles, organized around three pillars: data, evaluation, and methods. We first clarify the candidate task for- mulations and argue for a sensor-agnostic, instance-based definition that tolerates missing pre-event imagery. We then review existing datasets along the axes of sensing modality, temporal configuration, and geographic coverage. On the evaluation side, we show that prevailing practices, largely based on a single aggregate score, are theoretically fragile, and we lay the foundations of a more informative, ranking-based, multi-score protocol. We further review methods ranging from task-specific deep networks to recent Earth observation foundation models, noticing that generalization to unseen events and regions remains an open difficulty. Finally, we discuss how these elements can inform the organization of a BDA challenge, including cross-validation, challenge, and private data-sharing setups designed to build trust between developers and users.

Finite-Sample Guarantees for Fully Discrete Conditional Diffusion Models

Qiuyun Zhu • 2026-10-09

We establish finite-sample guarantees for conditional diffusion models learned from data and implemented with finitely many denoising steps. Our approach aligns the score-training objective with the sampling grid, allowing statistical estimation and numerical discretization errors to be analyzed together. Under a smooth conditional density model, we derive high-probability bounds on the generated distribution's error and quantify the required number of score evaluations. For every smoothness index $β>0$, a suitable grid yields averaged conditional Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence $\widetilde O_p(n^{-2β/(d_x+d_y+2β)})$ relative to the original target with or without early stopping. Both the full and early-stopped samplers attain average target total-variation (TV) error $\widetilde O_p(n^{-β/(d_x+d_y+2β)})$. Here $n$ is the sample size, $d_x,d_y$ are the data and conditioning dimensions, and errors are averaged over the conditioning variable. The total-variation bound matches the rate established in existing conditional distribution-estimation theory, up to logarithmic factors.

Tracing and Taming Hallucinated Terms via Subsequence-Association Filtering and Breadth-Aware Selection for LLM Query Expansion

Amit Kumar Jaiswal, Sagar Uprety, Prayag Tiwari • 2026-10-09

Large language models (LLMs) are now a standard source of query expansion terms but they hallucinate. They inject non existent entities and off-topic terms that add ranking noise and can hurt strong retrievers. Existing remedies such as corpus grounding, mutual verification and preference alignment treat hallucinated terms as an undifferentiated nuisance to be filtered by agreement heuristics, without a mechanistic account of why a given term appears or a principled criterion for how many terms to keep. We propose Subsequence-Association Filtered, breadth-aware query expansion (SAFE-QE), a training-free, retriever agnostic method that draws on the subsequence-association account of hallucination. We score each candidate expansion term by its corpus conditioned cross-context reproducibility, terms that reappear under randomised query contexts yet lack support in the pseudo-relevant set are flagged as products of dominant hallucinatory associations and down-weighted. Drawing on the characterisation of language generation as a trade-off between consistency (no hallucination) and breadth (coverage), we cast term selection as constrained coverage maximisation over the pseudo-relevant set, keeping a set that is approximately broad but consistent and stable across resampling. We also introduce the Hallucinated Expansion Term Rate, a query side faithfulness metric that counts injected expansion terms no relevant document supports. We evaluate our approach on benchmarked TREC and BEIR collections. Our SAFE-QE is statistically indistinguishable from generative and corpus-steered expansion on nDCG@10 while injecting fewer unsupported terms 4.3 per query against 27.8 for Query2doc and 57.5 for HyDE and on a strong learned sparse retriever, where every expansion method regresses. Faithfulness is therefore available at no measurable cost in ranking quality.

REFLECT: Role-Aware Detection of Sentiment, Emotion, Politeness, and Formality

Sangpil Youm, Annabel Stocks Natalias, Hayden Heninger, Leah Jones, Xiaoyang Jin, Xiaoya Zhang, Bonnie Dorr • 2026-10-09

Detecting communicative meaning in dialogue requires modeling not only lexical content but also the participant-role structures through which sentiment, emotion, politeness, and formality (SEPF) are expressed. Existing approaches rely on contextual language representations while leaving semantic roles implicit. We introduce a role-aware transformer architecture that maps the four SEPF dimensions to theory-grounded Semantic Role Labeling (SRL) configurations, incorporating these configurations directly into the transformer's self-attention mechanism. Role-aware attention models non-directional internal states (sentiment and emotion), while directional role-aware attention captures initiator-to-recipient relations for external cues (politeness and formality). The resulting role-aware representations are fused with contextual embeddings through a learned gate that adaptively weights how much role information each input contributes, rather than appending semantic roles as an auxiliary feature vector. Across four benchmark datasets, the framework yields statistically reliable gains over strong BERT-based baselines for emotion, politeness, and formality, with gains concentrated in theoretically predicted cases such as low-frequency, complex emotions and highly polite expressions; sentiment improvements are marginal and non-significant, suggesting that sentiment is conveyed more through lexical choice than participant-role structure. Additional evaluation on real-world data demonstrates that the framework improves both predictive performance and model traceability. These results demonstrate that linguistically grounded modifications to transformer self-attention improve the detection of communicative meaning, generalize beyond benchmark datasets to real-world mental-health data, and yield traceable, role-grounded explanations that extend beyond predictive performance to interpretability.

Improved screening of doped ZrO$_2$ catalysts for CO$_2$ hydrogenation from graph-based machine learning

Karsten Walz Jensen, Mie Andersen, Raffaele Cheula • 2026-10-09

Dopants can tune the activity of zirconia-based CO$_2$ hydrogenation catalysts through changes in adsorbate and transition-state (TS) energetics. Computing these quantities with density functional theory (DFT) becomes expensive when several dopants, reaction intermediates, and TSs must be screened. Here, we further develop the graph-based machine learning method Wasserstein Weisfeiler-Lehman Gaussian Process Regression (WWL-GPR): we introduce additional electronic-structure and local-environment features relevant for oxide surfaces and use the resulting model to predict adsorption and TS energies on ZrO$_2$(101) surfaces doped with Al$^{3+}$, Cd$^{2+}$, Ga$^{3+}$, In$^{3+}$, and Zn$^{2+}$. Adsorbate-surface structures are represented as attributed graphs that combine bonds connectivity and features. We benchmark WWL-GPR against thermodynamic scaling relations (TSR) for reaction intermediates and Bronsted-Evans-Polanyi relations (BEP) for TSs, and then propagate the predicted energies to microkinetic CH$_3$OH-yield estimates. WWL-GPR consistently improves the prediction of reaction intermediate energetics relative to linear scaling approaches and provides TS estimates that are competitive with BEP-based models. These improvements become especially relevant when the predicted energetics are propagated through the reaction network. Microkinetic calculations show that WWL-GPR-derived energies give CH$_3$OH yields closer to DFT-derived yields than the linear-relation workflow. Degree-of-rate-control analysis links the activity errors to dopant-dependent rate-controlling steps, including HCOO* formation, H$_2$COOH* dissociation, and H$_3$CO* formation. These results support WWL-GPR as a data-efficient surrogate for connecting atomistic energetics with catalyst-screening observables on doped oxide surfaces.

OccludeBench: Can VLMs Find What's Missing?

Jasin Cekinmez, Addison J. Wu, Ryo Mitsuhashi, Linrong Cai, Xingyu Fu, Zhuang Liu • 2026-10-09

How well can vision-language models (VLMs) reason about what is missing from an image? We study this question with OccludeBench, a manually curated benchmark for recovering fully occluded objects from the visual evidence that remains. OccludeBench distinguishes direct cases, where the hidden object must be recovered from a specific physical cue such as a shadow or reflection, from inferential cases, where it can be inferred from broader scene context. Across five VLMs, accuracy averages 42.7% on direct cases versus 62.7% on inferential cases, exhibiting a 20.0-point gap. Humans attain higher accuracies of 63.4% versus 68.5% for direct and inferential images, respectively, with no significant gap. To locate this failure, we introduce OccludeTrace, a four-stage diagnostic that isolates where the visual reasoning process breaks down. The bottleneck emerges when models must recover the relevant object from the projection within the full image: they succeed on only 50% of failed examples, compared with 91% of examples they answer correctly. Extended reasoning, tool use, and few-shot prompting yield inconsistent improvements and do not reliably eliminate the gap. In contrast, an inpaint-then-confront intervention, which prompts models to first generate a candidate hidden object and then evaluate that hypothesis against the visible physical cue, reduces the gap to a statistically non-significant difference for both models with image-generation capabilities. Together, our results establish a tractable, controlled diagnostic for isolating how VLMs reason about visual content that is missing from an image.

MARGoS: A Configuration-Driven Platform for Reproducible MARL Experiments in ARGoS

Tom Philipp Backert, Mohammad D. Soorati, Javad Ghofrani • 2026-10-09

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has become a prominent research direction, particularly within collective robotics, where the ARGoS simulator is widely used to study emergent swarm behavior. Yet despite this growth, systematic support for reuse and reproducibility in MARL-based swarm robotics remains limited and insufficiently standardized: a single experiment is assembled from manually aligned simulator files, training scripts, configuration values, paths, seeds, and environment details, so reuse and reproduction stay fragile even within one research group. To address this gap, we introduce MARGoS, a lightweight, configuration-driven platform developed in this project that elevates these experiment-defining inputs to first-class, platform-managed artifacts. MARGoS enables reproducible experimentation through configuration hashing, end-to-end seed propagation, frozen (immutable) configurations, structured logging, environment fingerprinting, and exportable experiment bundles that facilitate sharing, replay, and verification of results across machines and over time. A five-dimension evaluation (architecture, efficiency, reproducibility, usability, and collaboration) supports our claim that managing experiment variation as a platform responsibility makes ARGoS+MARL experiments easier to reuse, reproduce, and hand off. Our code is available at https://github.com/tombackert/margos.
