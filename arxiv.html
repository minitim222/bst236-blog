A first look at strange-quark polarization in top-quark decays with CMS Open Data

Yevgeny Kats • 2026-10-30

We present a first realization of strange-quark polarimetry in top-quark decays. The longitudinal polarization of $Λ$ baryons is measured in the strange jets of hadronic $W\to c\bar s$ decays in semileptonic top-quark pair events, using 16.39 fb$^{-1}$ of the public CMS data of pp collisions at $\sqrt{s}=13$ TeV. The $W$ decay produces the strange quark fully polarized, so that the $Λ$ polarization measures the transfer of the quark polarization in fragmentation. With the limited statistics available, our result is consistent both with zero and with the transfer inferred from the flavor-averaged LEP measurements. The statistical reach with the full Run-2, Run-3 and HL-LHC datasets is projected.

Instance-dependent sequential limits as an exact information ledger

Akshay Balsubramani • 2026-10-30

Instance-dependent limits for bandit regret, sequential tests, best-arm identification and Markov decision processes are proved by one chain. It runs from a divergence decomposition through data processing to a statistic, a testing step that turns a class guarantee into a requirement on the history, and optimization over alternatives and allocations. Only the first step is an identity. This work writes the chain as an exact ledger. By the chain rule, data processing loses exactly a conditional divergence, which vanishes for arms in natural exponential families at the per-arm counts and reward sums. A one-parameter family of martingale identities extends the decomposition to every tilt between two instances. The classical limits follow by relaxations with named defects. All defects but the asymptotic step's can be computed from an algorithm's own runs, the uniform quantification's only when the class guarantee is a number. On seven published sources, from movie ratings to engine replacement, the ledger locates the difference between standard algorithms and the classical limits. For identification among trained models and for Wald's test the largest term is usually the uniform quantification's.

Co-sufficient weighting: leveraging sparsity for more powerful variable selection in the linear model

Souhardya Sengupta, Lucas Janson • 2026-10-30

We show that for the problem of controlled variable selection in the Gaussian linear model, informative and p-value-independent weights (for weighted multiple testing) can be derived entirely from sufficient statistics and a belief in sparsity using only the data itself and no external quantitative side information. Using these weights, which we call \emph{co-sufficient weights} (CSW), in weighted versions of Holm's procedure and the Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) procedure results in substantially higher power than their unweighted analogues when the true coefficient vector is sparse, while retaining Holm's exact validity and BH's highly robust empirical validity regardless of sparsity. We demonstrate the superior performance of our proposed weighted methods compared to their unweighted analogues and other competing methods in a wide range of simulations and an application to HIV drug resistance.

SATFRONT: Detecting Atmospheric Fronts with Satellite Data using Machine Learning

Ioannis Dravilas, Miruna Stoicescu, Roope Tervo, Carlos Horn, Oriol Hinojo Comellas, Sina Montazeri, Andrea Meraner, Ana Lucic • 2026-10-30

Atmospheric fronts are associated with much of the high-impact weather in the mid-latitudes, yet their identification and classification remain largely manual and subjective. Existing deep learning approaches rely primarily on gridded reanalysis or forecast data, limiting their applicability to satellite observations available for time-critical applications. We introduce SATFRONT, a machine learning framework for detecting cold, warm, and occluded fronts over Europe from geostationary satellite observations, with reanalysis data as an optional additional input. The model is trained on frontal analyses from five European national meteorological services and evaluated against UK Met Office analyses for 2024. Using satellite observations alone, SATFRONT achieves a Critical Success Index (CSI) of 0.428 at a 150 km tolerance, within the range of agreement between analyses from different meteorological services (0.277-0.501) and higher than that of an operational rule-based front detector that uses satellite data (0.133). Adding ERA5 reanalysis increases the CSI to 0.591. ERA5 substantially improves the placement of individual fronts, although its effect on their annual spatial distribution varies by front type. These results demonstrate that cold, warm, and occluded fronts can be detected directly from geostationary satellite observations, providing a basis for automated frontal analyses at the high temporal frequency required for time-critical applications.

Hard Constraint Projection in a Physics Informed Neural Network (Burgers)

Miranda J S Horne, Peter K Jimack, Amirul Khan, He Wang • 2026-10-30

Physics informed machine learning provides a framework to study and simulate fluid dynamics, potentially at a fraction of the computational cost of traditional numerical methods, with the goal of improving the explainability, accuracy and robustness of purely data driven machine learning methods. In this work, we extend the hard constraint projection (HCP) method developed by Chen et al. [Journal of Computational Physics, 445, 110624, 2021] from a linear partial differential equation (PDE) to a nonlinear PDE: the 1D Burgers' equation. To strictly impose a discrete approximation of the governing equation, the Burgers' equation is first locally discretised (using a finite difference stencil) then decomposed into a constraint matrix and a prediction matrix. Using tools from linear algebra, the constraint matrix is used to construct a projection matrix. A physics informed neural network (PINN) [Raissi et al. Journal of Computational Physics, 378, 686, 2019] is used to predict the velocity, and a final unlearnable layer projects the velocity prediction to the hyperplane of exact solutions to a discretised form of the Burgers' equation. Our initial results indicate that the HCP-PINN initially trains similarly to an equivalent PINN, and as the PINN loss plateaus, the HCP-PINN loss continues to decline. When tested on a previously unseen uniform grid of points covering the domain, the HCP-PINN exhibits higher accuracy, converging to a data error approximately an order of magnitude lower than the comparable PINN. We also observe that the inclusion of the HCP layer results in better observance of the governing PDE. Our findings suggest that the HCP-PINN can be more robust to the selection of physics collocation points, and less dependent on collocation point quantity.

CausalWAM: Learning the Causes of Actions in World Action Models

Juntao Cheng, Jinzhou Tang, Fan Feng, Henrik I. Christensen, Yujia Zheng • 2026-10-30

World action models (WAMs) learn latent variables from observations and use these to both predict the evolution of the scene and to generate actions. The prediction has to be of the whole scene, whereas only a portion of the scene causes actions. WAMs fitted to demonstrations do not, in general, guarantee that they will generate actions solely from the latent causes of the demonstrated actions (the action-relevant latent variables), as the model could just as well learn to imitate in this way whilst entangling the action-relevant latent variables with the nuisance variables. We therefore separate out what causes action from what prediction depends on. We prove that under a sparsity constraint the latent variables used to generate actions are an invertible function of the action-relevant latent variables alone and the true structure is identified up to a permutation of the latent variables. This requires neither sufficient temporal dependence nor any assumptions on how the unobserved regimes evolve and holds even when the action-relevant latent variables are statistically dependent on the rest. We therefore propose CWAM (Causal WAM), which incorporates the constraint into a pretrained WAM by adding a sparse routing path to its action head, with the world modeling objective remaining unchanged. Experiments on both synthetic data and real-world robots validate the theory as well as showcase the improved efficiency in closed-loop success.

Landscape Intervals: A Geometric Framework for Multiparameter Persistence Landscapes

Pan Fang, Washington Mio, Tom Needham • 2026-10-30

Classical persistent homology provides a summary of the multiscale topology of a filtration of a space. These summaries are not naturally valued in a vector space, limiting their direct use in standard machine learning pipelines. To address this issue, Bubenik introduced persistence landscapes, vector-valued invariants of persistence modules, which operate by conveniently encoding the behavior of rank functions of modules along the parameter axis. When passing to multiparameter persistent homology, changes in the rank functions vary by different amounts in different directions. In this paper, we introduce the landscape interval, a subset of the parameter space of a multiparameter persistence module which records changes in the rank function in all directions simultaneously. This geometric perspective leads to two novel invariants which serve as notions of persistence landscapes for multiparameter modules. We show that our invariants generalize existing landscape-type invariants: in particular, they recover Bubenik's landscapes in the one-parameter setting and allow us to obtain the multiparameter landscapes introduced by Vipond. For our main theoretical contributions, we establish two exact identities connecting landscapes with erosion distances, prove that our invariants are stable under perturbations, and refine the interleaving-stability bounds for the existing landscape-type invariants. We also show that on a certain class of multiparameter persistence modules, the full invariants can be recovered from finite information about the module. On the practical side, we introduce a robust numerical framework for extracting our invariants from real data and for incorporating them into standard machine learning pipelines. The framework is illustrated on surface mesh and medical image data.

The Knowledge Layer Model (KLM): Evidence-Visible AI Output, Routing Diagnostics, and Negative Results

Omer Asim Ozturk, Cihan Aydiner • 2026-10-30

AI answers require more than apparent correctness: reviewers need to inspect their recorded basis. We introduce the Knowledge Layer Model (KLM), an open, implementation-neutral specification for inference records with versioned epistemic assessments, evidence relations, and explicit unknowns. SEDIM, the knowledge and evidence architecture of Nage AI OS, is a reference implementation. We reference KLM v0.3 (repository tag v0.3.0). Empirical material consists of separate internal experiments, not end-to-end validation. A historical platform-domain routing benchmark reports 137/150 classifications across 15 classes (91.3%; nominal 95% Wilson interval 85.7-94.9%); the numerator is recovered from a rounded summary, and historical example membership is unverified. A native-model weight-path configuration yielded 0/15 successful answers in a corrected two-source test. A capacity-growth comparison could not establish a reliable effect because of a defective legacy scorer and missing original outputs. Lexical and scoped diagnostics each yielded AUC 0.008 for silent-versus-contradicted examples, below the registered minimum of 0.65. Legacy packing lost gold-containing text in 118/221 selected cases; repaired packing retained it in 221/221. These measure text presence, not answer quality. Python and TypeScript verifiers reportedly agreed on six checks per synthetic fixture; a separate production disclosure was not accepted. Restoration, re-derivation, human-rubric support, boundary isolation, and bounded-response evaluations remain proposed. Findings are internal and author-reported; historical observations are distinguished from later reproductions. Evidence visibility does not establish correctness, causal reliance, security, or regulatory compliance. Independent replication and a complete public audit package remain future work.

Rapid mixing of Potts Glauber dynamics throughout uniqueness

Antonio Blanca, Reza Gheissari, Heehyun Park, Xusheng Zhang • 2026-10-30

Sampling from the ferromagnetic $q$-state Potts model is a fundamental problem in statistical physics and approximate counting. Although computationally hard in the worst case, sampling is expected to be efficient throughout the high-temperature uniqueness regime of the model using the natural single-site Glauber dynamics. We establish fast mixing bounds for every $q$, in the full high-temperature regimes in the two central graph families on $n$ vertices. (1) For every $Δ\ge 3$, the Potts Glauber dynamics mixes in $n\operatorname{polylog}(n)$ steps with high probability on a uniformly random $Δ$-regular graph for all $β<β_u(q,\mathbb T_Δ)$ where $β_u(q,\mathbb T_Δ)$ is the uniqueness threshold on the $Δ$-regular tree; (2) For every $d\ge 2$, the Potts Glauber dynamics has inverse spectral gap $n^{1+o(1)}$ on cubes or tori in $\mathbb Z^d$ for all $β<β_c(q,\mathbb Z^d)$ where $β_c(q,\mathbb Z^d)$ is the critical point on $\mathbb Z^d$. Both temperature thresholds are sharp as the Glauber dynamics slows down exponentially above them. These results overcome the challenge of bounding mixing times without access to correlation decay bounds in the presence of pinnings (i.e., without spectral independence or strong spatial mixing). For this purpose, our proof introduces a new dichotomy principle: throughout the high-temperature regime, any initial spin distribution is either such that its pinnings ensure good correlation decay, or Glauber dynamics escapes from that distribution quickly. We expect this kind of dichotomy to have further applications. As a demonstration, we also develop its analog for random-cluster dynamics and use it to prove that Swendsen-Wang dynamics on random $Δ$-regular graphs mixes in $O(\log n)$ time throughout uniqueness. The results in this paper were arrived at with the assistance of ChatGPT 5.6 and 6.

Variance-adaptive Chernoff bounds: nonasymptotically valid, asymptotically sharp confidence sets for unbounded means

Aaditya Ramdas, Diego Martinez-Taboada • 2026-10-30

Consider the classical statistical problem of sub-Gaussian mean estimation under martingale dependence: given $n$ observations with conditional mean $μ$ and a known conditional sub-Gaussian proxy $s>0$, we seek a nonasymptotically valid confidence interval for $μ$. The usual Chernoff interval has half-width $s\sqrt{2\log(2/α)/n}$; it neither adapts to the actual standard deviation $σ\le s$ nor attains the Gaussian quantile $z_{1-α/2}$. We construct an analytically closed-form nonasymptotically-valid interval under martingale dependence whose endpoints, at every iid law with $σ> 0$, agree almost surely to first order with $\bar X_n\pmσz_{1-α/2}/\sqrt n$. The two new building blocks are a sub-Gaussian exponential supermartingale with an \emph{empirical} quadratic variance and a cubic correction term, and a sub-Gaussian continuation supermartingale, the former delivering variance adaptivity ("Empirical-Bernstein'' for unbounded data), the latter also providing the Gaussian quantile ("Empirical-Berry-Esseen'' for unbounded data). The same constructions yield a closed-form time-uniform confidence sequence with asymptotically unimprovable half-width $σ\sqrt{2\log\log n/n}$. We also give closed-form multivariate ellipsoids with the true-covariance chi-square limit, and extend the scalar Gaussian and LIL constructions to handle heavy-tailed settings where one only knows a bound on any absolute central moment of order greater than two. Experiments show substantial width reductions when the true variance is smaller than the implied variance bound, at small loss in the boundary cases. The surprising (to us) takeaway message is: given any more than two moments, one can construct fully data dependent nonasymptotic confidence sets for the mean that are asymptotically efficient.

Valid GMM Inference with Non-parametrically Generated Discrete Type

Taegyu Yang • 2026-10-30

The recent progress in machine learning and classification methods has enabled researchers to predict economic discrete attributes that are not observed in standard datasets. These range from missing demographics, such as ethnicity and poverty status, to economic categories, such as innovative firms, metropolitan areas, occupational groups, binary treatment status, and counterfactual status. Although the plug-in procedure is widely used in empirical applications, nonparametric first-stage estimation recovering the labels can introduce regularization bias because of its slow convergence rate. Even if the double/debiased machine learning approach provides a way to correct the regularization bias, the according bias-correction term is not well defined in our problem, because even a small first-stage error can change the type discontinuously. To address this problem, we smooth the discontinuous type indicators using a smooth kernel function and a shrinking tuning parameter $b$. We then construct a well-defined Neyman-orthogonal score and define the appropriate debiased estimator. We show that this debiased estimator satisfies $\sqrt{Nb}$-consistency and asymptotic normality under regularity conditions. The slower $\sqrt{Nb}$ convergence rate is the cost of correcting the regularization bias when the nonparametric first-stage learner enters the moment function through discontinuous functions.

Benchmarking Single-Photon Sources for Measurement-Device-Independent Quantum Key Distribution

Janka Memmen, Yvonne Lasars, Tobias Heindel, Nathan Walk, Anna Pappa • 2026-10-30

Measurement-Device-Independent Quantum Key Distribution (MDI-QKD) enables secure communication without trusting the measurement devices, but its performance critically depends on the quality of the employed photon sources. We compare MDI-QKD implementations based on Weak Coherent Pulses (WCPs) with those employing Quantum Dot (QD) single-photon sources. WCP-based schemes are fundamentally limited by Poissonian photon-number statistics, imposing a trade-off between single- and multi-photon emissions, whereas QD sources exhibit sub-Poissonian statistics with low antibunching parameter $g^{(2)}(0)$. Using a decoy-state analysis for both source types, we find that even at moderate mean photon numbers, QD-based implementations outperform the best-case WCP configuration - where the mean photon number is optimised at each transmission distance - across the full range of transmission distances. We further incorporate the effect of imperfect photon indistinguishability via Hong-Ou-Mandel interference visibilities into the security framework and demonstrate that both source types are highly sensitive to even small deviations from ideal indistinguishability, significantly affecting achievable key rates and maximum transmission distance.

Empirical Evaluation of Quantum Kernel Methods for Classification: A Comparative Study with Classical Kernels

Ronak Bhalgami • 2026-10-30

Quantum kernel methods are the most concrete near-term proposal for quantum machine learning on classical data, yet published benchmarks disagree on whether they outperform tuned classical kernels, and the disagreement cannot be resolved from the literature because the comparisons are not protocol-controlled. We argue that the obstacle is one of measurement, not theory: the usual setup fixes the quantum feature map's data scaling while grid-searching the classical kernel's hyperparameters, recomputes nothing per fold, and reports cost in simulator time. We therefore treat the comparison itself as the object of study and impose three controls. The bandwidth is tuned on the same grid budget as the classical $γ$, the kernel is recomputed inside every fold from fold-fitted preprocessing, and cost is counted in hardware circuit evaluations. On top of these we run a factorial design of 324 cells in which dimensionality, training size, class separability and data structure are explicit covariates, together with four standard datasets, a shots-versus-gate-noise ablation, and a cost sweep. The dominant effect we measure is not quantum: tuning the feature map's bandwidth is worth 0.120 accuracy on synthetic data and 0.339 on real data, exceeding every quantum-classical difference we observe. Tuned quantum kernels are then statistically indistinguishable from a tuned RBF on all four real datasets. The advantage on synthetic data is confined almost entirely to data generated by the feature map itself, and it shrinks with dimensionality (-0.0045 per qubit) while growing with sample size. Finite sampling costs an order of magnitude more accuracy than gate and readout noise, and a fidelity kernel needs $5\times10^{7}$ circuits at n = 10,000. Protocol variance is thus sufficient to explain the field's contradictory conclusions.

This paper presents TurnTurk-GPT, a text-based turn-taking predictor for Turkish obtained by fine-tuning a Turkish GPT-2 model with the TurnGPT objective on synthetic and real dialogue, and evaluates it on real Turkish conversation against rule baselines, a zero-shot Turkish language model and a widely used multilingual end-of-turn detector. Evaluation uses word-level decision points on unpunctuated, lower-cased text approximating the output of streaming speech recognisers, recording-disjoint and speaker-disjoint folds, nested threshold selection and paired cluster bootstrap tests. Synthetic dialogue alone yields the weakest trained model, and when a speaker pauses the zero-shot and multilingual baselines remain close to chance. The model and the evaluation protocol are publicly released.

On Statistical-Computational Gaps in Graph Combinatorial Testing Problems

Ikjun Choi, Pratik Patil, Alessandro Rinaldo • 2026-10-30

We study high-dimensional graph combinatorial hypothesis testing problems in which a signal is supported on the edge set of an unknown subgraph drawn from a class invariant under vertex relabeling. For Gaussian observations, we characterize sharp statistical and computational thresholds (at polynomial order) for the signal magnitude required for strong detection across various combinatorial graph alternatives. Our analysis is based on bounding the second moments of the full and low-degree likelihood ratios in terms of the overlap of two independent latent structures and comparing the resulting lower bounds with scan, average, and coordinatewise maximum tests. Under an appropriate low-degree conjecture for computational hardness, we obtain planted-clique thresholds matching those known for Gaussian submatrix detection and identify new statistical-computational gaps for multiple planted cliques and hypercliques, thick cycles and thick paths. We also establish a novel transfer principle across natural exponential family observation models: matching the one-coordinate chi-square divergence preserves full likelihood-ratio second moments exactly and makes low-degree second moments equivalent up to constant factors under a degree condition. Under local regularity, we obtain corresponding testing upper bounds in terms of an effective signal strength. When these upper and lower bounds match, they yield universal threshold scales in the local alternative regime. As a consequence, statistical-computational gaps for planted-clique detection in Erdős--Rényi settings can be deduced from the Gaussian model.

FoAViS: Analyzing Learned Fixation Strategies in a Foveated Active Vision System

Valentin Hassler, Jonathan Henrich, Yohaï-Eliel Berreby, Haoze Zhu, Alexander Ecker • 2026-10-30

Human vision is foveated and active: resolution is highest at the fovea and falls off towards the periphery, and fixations are actively directed to locations of interest. Building a machine system that processes information in the same way lets us study gaze behavior in a controlled setting where the system's components and objectives are known. The emergence of gaze characteristics can therefore be examined systematically, which may in turn inform our understanding of biological vision. This requires a model that can sample like the fovea, handle various tasks from classification to dense prediction, and be trained concurrently with a fixation policy. In our study, we present such a system and study its capabilities and the fixation policies that emerge from it. Our system, FoAViS, samples visual input by adopting the established log-polar foveation and accumulates information in a 2D spatial memory. Due to its 2D structure, the same memory can be read out globally for classification or densely for semantic segmentation, without any architectural change. Fixations are drawn from a 2D map by a policy trained concurrently with the task. We find that learned fixation policies select more informative, task-specific locations than non-learned heuristics. These policies also shape the task representations in a beneficial way: for both classification and segmentation, models trained concurrently with a policy outperform models trained with random locations, even when the latter are provided with learned scanpaths. Moreover, these scanpaths exhibit similarities to human gaze behavior by capturing aspects of empirical scanpath characteristics. Our work marks an important step away from standard passive, single-pass architectures, towards biologically grounded models of vision. It opens a path for studying goal-directed gaze behavior and information representation in the visual system.

Shock Loading Accesses Reaction Pathways beyond Accelerated Thermolysis

Mingjie Wen, Jiahe Han, Zihan Zhou, Hao Wang, Xueyong Guo, Qingzhao Chu, Dongping Chen • 2026-10-30

Thermolysis and shock loading can produce similar major stable products in energetic materials, despite different chemical evolution and reaction pathways. Here, we develop EMFF-MACE, a reactive machine-learning potential for C/H/N/O energetic materials, enabling thermolysis simulations of 20 materials and shock simulations of 10 on a common potential energy surface. Comparisons at matched normalized reaction progress reveal divergent chemical states and local structural environments. Mean reaction-state similarity decreases from 94.0% at 20% progress to 25.9% at trajectory endpoints, while 47.1-92.6% of shock local environments lie outside corresponding material-specific thermolysis reference domains. Structural and reaction-network analyses reveal enhanced intermolecular reorganization and additional association and rearrangement branches under shock loading, despite partially shared early decomposition chemistry. These findings show that loading history influences the structural states and subsequent reaction pathways accessed during decomposition, rather than merely the rate of thermolysis.

Locality, Probability and the Relevance of History

Sebastian Horvat • 2026-10-30

Relativistic theory and experiments teach us that causal influences are constrained by the speed of light. Deterministic theories incorporate this lesson in $\textit{functional}$ terms: whether an event occurs or not is functionally determined by events that occur in its immediate causal past. In this paper we suggest a natural generalization of this constraint that applies to probabilistic theories: the $\textit{probability}$ that an event occurs is to be functionally determined by the $\textit{probability}$ of the event-occurrences in its immediate causal past. This constraint differs from Bell's criterion of local causality and turns out to imply it only under an additional assumption that requires history to be, in a sense, $\textit{ir}$relevant. Bell-inequality-violating quantum phenomena are thus arguably $\textit{not}$ in tension with relativity, locality, "statistical independence", nor with a supposed "realism". What they instead show is that history is, in a sense, $\textit{relevant}$.

Generative Design and Graph Neural Network Screening of Novel Bimetallic Alloy Catalysts for the Hydrogen Evolution Reaction

Vipin Kurian Elavunkel, Sylvain Le Tonquesse, Hadrien Vroylandt • 2026-10-30

Identifying earth-abundant catalysts for the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) is a central challenge in scalable green hydrogen production. We present a computational pipeline that couples diffusion-based generative modelling, machine-learning force-field relaxation, and graph neural network (GNN) property prediction to discover bimetallic HER catalysts beyond the structures and stoichiometries covered by existing adsorption databases. A generative model (MatterGen) is fine-tuned on bulk alloy structures labelled with their minimum hydrogen adsorption energy and steered towards adsorption energies near zero. From the generated pool we retain the stable structures that are novel bimetallic alloys: binary compositions built from the training-set metals but at stoichiometries or structures absent from that database. Each candidate's low-index facets are ranked by a machine-learning-force-field (MatterSim) surface-energy screen, and only the most stable terminations are converted into explicit slab models with hydrogen adsorbed at all high-symmetry surface sites and relaxed with the same force field. Two GNN surrogates (MEGNet and ALIGNN), trained on a high-throughput density functional theory (DFT) dataset, then screen the resulting slab candidates. Because the two models encode the local environment through different inductive biases, we retain only the alloys on which both agree, reducing the influence of any single model. This consensus screening, combined with a MatterSim energy-difference triangulation, resulted in a shortlist of near-thermoneutral bimetallic candidates. Eight are validated with plane-wave DFT, and four of them, Pd5Au, Pt5Rh, Cu5Pt3, and Pd13Ru3, bind hydrogen within the thermoneutral window. All eight alloys are dynamically stable by phonon calculations. These four alloys are highlighted as DFT-validated bimetallic HER candidates for experimental follow-up.

In this work, we study the turbulent origin of ion-scale Current Sheets (CSs) and the dissipation of energy via magnetic reconnection. We analyze 103 hours of high-cadence (4 samples/s) in-situ ion data from Solar Orbiter, collected between July 2020 and September 2022. We apply an automatic detection algorithm that finds current sheets (CSs) of thickness (length in the normal direction, $λ$) near the ion inertial length ($δ_i$), and classifies them as Alfvénic or reconnecting. We compile a catalog of 974 CSs of thickness in the range of few to 90 ion inertial lengths, of which 53 contain an ion reconnection jet.
