Parametric neural-network wavefunctions across a bosonic phase transition

Daniil S. Antonenko, Liang Fu, Leonid I. Glazman • 2026-11-24

We propose Boseformer, a universal attention-based neural-network architecture for representing the wavefunctions of particles with bosonic quantum statistics, including superfluid and crystal phases. The model is trained to generate the wavefunction as a function of a Hamiltonian parameter across the quantum phase transition between these phases. Once trained with variational Monte Carlo optimization, it can serve as a foundation model describing the phase transition without the need to retrain it for individual parameter values. We demonstrate our method on a system of two-dimensional bosons with $1/r^3$ repulsive interaction.

Anomalies and the Berry Phase: From $π^0\toγγ$ to the geometry of the fermion vacuum

Spenta R. Wadia • 2026-11-24

We review the chiral anomaly from a historical perspective, from the $π^0\toγγ$ puzzle and the work of Adler, Bell and Jackiw, to the Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) term. We then describe how the WZW term was motivated from quantum chromodynamics in 1983-85. Integrating out the non-Abelian gauge fields, that have a gapped spectrum, gives a Nambu-Jona-Lasinio type theory of quarks, and integrating out the quarks, which are gapped by chiral symmetry breaking, gives the WZW model. The fermion determinant splits into a regulator-dependent modulus, which gives the dynamics of mesons, and a universal phase, which is fixed by the anomaly and is the WZW term. Restricted to the collective coordinates of the baryon soliton, the WZW term imposes a U(1) constraint that makes the baryon wave functions monopole harmonics and fixes their spin, statistics and flavour multiplets. We then explain that this phase is a Berry phase: the Schwinger terms of current algebra are the curvature of a U(1) connection on the space of chiral field configurations, and the WZW term is its holonomy, which we call a Berry-Wilson loop. In 1+1 dimensions we write the connection explicitly in terms of Euler angles, exhibiting a Dirac monopole whose quantised charge is the level of the current algebra.

Anharmonic enhancement of thermal conductivity in amorphous Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$: A consistent picture from molecular dynamics and Wigner transport

Luiz Felipe C. Pereira, Kamil Iwanowski, Michele Simoncelli, Omar Abou El Kheir, Marco Bernasconi, Riccardo Mazzarello, Francesco Mauri • 2026-11-24

Anharmonicity limits heat conduction in crystals, but its role in amorphous solids, where heat is carried mainly by tunneling between quasi-degenerate vibrations, remains unsettled. Here we disentangle the contributions of structural disorder, quantum statistics, and anharmonicity to the thermal conductivity of amorphous Ge$_2$Sb$_2$Te$_5$ (aGST), the prototypical phase-change memory material. Using a single machine-learned potential trained on first-principles data, we compare classical equilibrium and nonequilibrium molecular dynamics (MD), which include anharmonicity to all orders, with harmonic Allen-Feldman theory and the anharmonic Wigner transport equation, both of which include quantum statistics. Four MD approaches agree on a conductivity of 0.171-0.181 W m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ at room temperature, about 16% above the harmonic Allen-Feldman value for the same model, and the Wigner formulation bridges the two: with classical statistics, it agrees with MD to within 5%. We trace this anharmonic enhancement to vibrational couplings that grow with the frequency difference between modes, so that anharmonic broadening opens additional channels for heat conduction. A comparison with silica, alumina, and disordered carbons reveals three regimes, in which anharmonicity reduces, barely affects, or enhances the conductivity. The predicted glass-like increase of the conductivity with temperature is consistent with thermoreflectance measurements. These results show that anharmonicity has no universal effect in disordered solids, and can promote rather than limit heat transport in strongly disordered materials.

The Radar-based Activities of Daily Living (R-ADL) Dataset

Dominique Regli, Brian C. Williams • 2026-11-24

Monitoring Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) supports safety and well-being for people living at home, especially older adults, and radar sensing is gaining increasing attention in the field of activity recognition for its ability to ensure privacy in the home while also surpassing technical limitations of other sensing modalities. However, radar-based activity recognition remains constrained by the limited availability of large-scale datasets. We introduce the Radar-based Activities of Daily Living (R-ADL) dataset to addresses the gaps identified in existing work: 1) it expands the scale of available data by providing over eight hours of annotated recordings in which a person performs eight distinct activities; 2) it overcomes room configuration limitations by recording from five different locations in a two-bedroom, two-bathroom apartment; and 3) it extends beyond primitive actions to focus on high-level ADLs. In addition to the dataset, this paper introduces practical guidance for future ADL dataset construction. We conducted a detailed analysis of how ADLs are prioritized across academic research, health survey tools, and clinical trials to identify clinically significant ADLs. Furthermore, we provide our experimental design, data collection and annotation strategies, post-processing pipeline for forming the R-ADL dataset from raw data, and baseline results on standard machine learning architectures. The R-ADL dataset serves as a valuable resource for advancing ADL recognition systems as we continue building larger and more diverse datasets.

Pragmatic Deeply Debiased ML

Victor Chernozhukov, Chengchun Shi • 2026-11-24

Debiased machine learning (DML) combines a fitted outcome regression with weighted residuals to remove first-order regularization bias when estimating parameters such as average treatment effects, yet a second-order bias remains whenever regression and weighting errors align. For linear regression functionals, we target this second-order bias by training a strong learner to predict first-stage residuals from many covariates, yielding a one-dimensional (or low-dimensional) working model whose bias contribution is estimated on an independent sample. To complement this targeted search, we also analyze general kernel-based corrections, including covariate partitions and smoothers. We prove that deeply debiased ML is algebraically identical to ordinary DML evaluated with a post-hoc regression update that adjusts each scored observation using kernel-weighted residuals from the remaining evaluation observations. In our empirical and simulation studies, strong initial DML learners often leave the second-order correction small relative to sampling uncertainty, in which case the correction serves as a diagnostic check alongside sensitivity bounds for any remaining misspecification bias.

U-Statistics-Powered LLM Reasoning

Shijin Gong, Kai Ye, Victor Chernozhukov, Xinyu Zhang, Chengchun Shi • 2026-11-24

Since its introduction by DeepSeek, group relative policy optimization (GRPO) has been widely adopted for training large reasoning models and agents, spurring a proliferation of its variants. Yet the group size, a crucial hyperparameter in these GRPO-type algorithms, remains largely chosen by trial and error. Exploiting the U-statistic structure of GRPO's policy gradient, we recast the selection of the group size as a classical statistical problem of variance ratio estimation. This problem is particularly challenging when the denominator of the ratio is small, rendering naive plug-in estimators unstable. To address this challenge, we propose a pessimistic ratio estimator that accounts for the denominator's estimation uncertainty. We characterise the statistical properties of this estimator across three sample-size regimes. Empirically, using %a jackknife-based pessimistic rule on synthetic tasks from Reasoning Gym and real-world benchmarks covering both mathematical reasoning and code generation, we show that our adaptive group size selection reduces the mean squared error of the resulting policy gradient and improves policy optimisation over fixed-group-size baselines, with modest additional computational cost.

Class-resolved sifting for quantum key distribution with photon pairs hyperentangled in polarization and transverse spatial variables

Murilo H. Magiotto, Millena B. Novaes, Wesley B. Cardoso, Ardiley T. Avelar, Guilherme L. Zanin, Rafael M. Gomes • 2026-11-24

Photon pairs hyperentangled in polarization and transverse spatial variables can carry two key bits per pair in entanglement-based quantum key distribution. We develop class-resolved sifting for such sources: every detected pair is assigned to a class according to which degrees of freedom were measured in compatible bases, and error and key rates are estimated separately in each class. Applied to previously reported data from a two-crystal spontaneous parametric down-conversion source read out in intermediate fractional-Fourier planes, the method recovers the key yield of independent sifting and turns the comparison between classes into a quantitative consistency test for cross-dependencies between the two degrees of freedom. The measured dependence lies below the spatial-polarization coupling predicted for the source, at the edge of the present statistical sensitivity. Resolving the data by polarization-analyzer setting, we find an approximately twofold dependence of the spatial error on the polarization setting in one readout arm, attributable to the measurement apparatus rather than to the source and relevant to basis-dependent side channels. We compare the hyperentangled protocol with a single 4-dimensional system measured in two mutually unbiased bases. Fundamentally, both yield the same key per detected pair and obey the same entropic bound, but they differ in their tensor-product structure: hyperentanglement factors the bases into separate two-outcome measurements, which are simpler to implement and allow errors to be attributed to each degree of freedom, whereas a single variable gives access to non-product bases that can increase the noise tolerance. For the present source, whose polarization coherence is limited by partial which-crystal erasure, a single-variable spatial encoding would yield more key only if its four-outcome readout kept the symbol error within a few percent.

A Bernstein-Type Variance-Adaptive PMW Mechanism for DP Query Release

Mohamed Aniss Benstaali, Habib Louafi, Naima Bessah • 2026-11-24

Private Multiplicative Weights (PMW) is a central mechanism for the differentially private release of adaptive linear statistics over a finite universe. Its classical analysis uses a fixed multiplicative-weights learning rate and does not exploit the local variance of the current hypothesis. This paper presents PMW-Bernstein, a variance-adaptive refinement in which the update step is determined by the relationship between the privately estimated query error and the variance under the current hypothesis. The mechanism retains the standard PMW privacy architecture based on Sparse Vector screening, Gaussian numerical measurements, sequential composition, and post-processing. Through a potential-based analysis, we prove a one-step progress bound that yields a global weighted energy bound, recovers the classical $O(1/α2)$ update regime in general, and establishes an $O(1/α)$ update regime under a Bernsteintype condition on updated rounds. Controlled simulations over synthetic finite-universe distributions and query workloads show that the adaptive rule reduces the number of updates in the low-variance regime, remains competitive in the high-variance regime, and succeeds within the prescribed update budget in the heterogeneous regime where the fixed-step baseline fails.

The Poissonian limit of a model inspired by the one-dimensional ERAP, and its application to satellite data

Matteo D'Achille, Jessie Levillain, Pascal Moyal, Achim Schlather • 2026-11-24

We consider a model of particle tracking in one dimension studied by Quarck in which $n$ independent random points of the interval $[0,1]$ are shifted by i.i.d. noise at a common scale $\varepsilon_n>0$. We prove that, if $\varepsilon_n=αn^{-2}+\mathbf{o}(n^{-2})$ for some $α>0$ when $n\to \infty$, then in the latter limit the total number of consecutive swaps in the shifted sample w.r.t. its order statistics converges in distribution to a Poisson random variable with a parameter depending explicitly on the product of $α$, the mean absolute difference of the shifts and the $L^2$-norm of the density of points. We compare the statistical criterion implied by this theorem to numerical simulations based on real terrain maps, and discuss both its usefulness and limitations.

Score-Based Diffusion for Hypergraph Signals in the t-Spectral Domain

Zhiyuan Dong, Carlos Mundo-Levano, Daniel L. Lau, Gonzalo R. Arce • 2026-11-24

We introduce the first score-based generative model for signals on a fixed hypergraph, formulated in the spectral domain of t-product hypergraph signal processing (t-HGSP). The naive tensor heat semigroup is ill-posed, since after palindromic symmetrization the spectral slices of the Laplacian tensor are no longer positive semidefinite. A per-mode frequency shift repairs it, yielding a forward Ornstein-Uhlenbeck diffusion that decouples per (eigenmode, slice) pair with closed-form marginals, exact score targets, and an exactly isotropic stationary law. A lightweight slice-conditioned denoiser is trained on the resulting real spectral grid. Sampling combines its score on data-active modes with the closed-form score elsewhere, which proves essential for stability at scale. A closed cosine-form observation operator turns missing-node recovery into linear-Gaussian guidance with stabilized posterior sampling. On hypergraphs of up to 500 nodes, our model beats a clique-expansion baseline by margins growing from $2\times$ to $27\times$ in covariance error, matches the graph-spectral statistics of the data better than the graph-basis model itself, and reaches the finite-sample estimation floor on data rotated off the t-spectral axes. Posterior sampling reduces missing-node NMSE to 0.28-0.64 (classical baselines 0.87-1.1) at conservative coverage, and cuts the framework's own denoising baseline by up to $3.6\times$.

Energy transport from an active medium

Simon Krekels, Christian Maes, Ion Santra • 2026-11-24

We study the energy current through a harmonic chain whose left end is coupled, via a short-range contact potential, to a medium of overdamped run-and-tumble particles moving on a ring, while the right end is attached to a zero-temperature Langevin reservoir. The activity is parametrized by the propulsion speed $v_0$ and the persistence time $τ$. Using nonequilibrium response theory at weak system-medium coupling, we predict the stationary current and simulations reproduce it over a large part of the $(v_0,τ)$ plane. That includes the possible nonmonotonicity of the current as function of $v_0$ and $τ$, and occurrence of resonances with the normal modes of the chain, most significantly at large $τ$ and small $v_0$. There is a critical propulsion speed $v_c$ at which the ring-crossing frequency of the particles leaves the phonon band: for $v_0<v_c$ the current grows with persistence, while for $v_0>v_c$ it decays as $(v_0^2τ)^{-1}$. We also explain where the theoretical prediction fails, especially due to an oscillatory instability originating from a sufficiently strong negative friction induced on the leftmost oscillator. Finally, the persistent medium produces strongly non-Gaussian velocity statistics and long-range, oscillating velocity correlations in the bulk of the chain.

Cell and nuclear shapes predict confined motility

Malcolm Mehrabian-Terlexis, Jacqueline Janssen, Paul Sitoleux, Thierry Mora, Aleksandra M. Walczak, Cécile Sykes • 2026-11-24

Cancer metastasis relies on an enhanced property of cells to migrate efficiently even through constrictions smaller than their nucleus. Both cell and nuclear morphology may influence this process. How these morphologies relate to the likelihood that a cell will transit through a constriction remains unclear. Here we combine microfluidic experiments with machine learning to predict whether MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells are able to transit through narrow constrictions. Cells migrate within vertically confined boxes, and may migrate into neighboring boxes by transiting through constrictions, requiring a deformation of the nucleus. Defining a set of 12 geometrical features derived from cell and nuclear contours, we find that their average values are not statistically indicative of whether or not a cell will transit. To overcome this limitation, we use trajectories to train classifiers and evaluate their ability to predict future transit events. We find that the cell area and cell circularity carry most of the predictive information and less circular cells are more likely to transit. Without retraining, the models also predict transit through narrower constrictions and in more adhesive substrates. Morphological features of cells about 30 minutes before a transit event proved most informative about future transit events occurring or not. Together, our results show that cell shape, readable from simple contour descriptors, contains a transferable but condition-dependent signature of imminent transit.

Estimating GPU Roofline Arithmetic Intensity with Off-the-Shelf LLMs and Compiler Artifacts

Gregory Bolet, Giorgis Georgakoudis, Konstantinos Parasyris, Harshitha Menon, Niranjan Hasabnis, Kirk W. Cameron, Gal Oren • 2026-11-24

GPU developers use profiling to understand computation and data movement, but access to target hardware can delay optimization. We investigate whether off-the-shelf large language models (LLMs) can estimate Roofline arithmetic intensity (the ratio of floating-point operations (FLOPs) to device-memory bytes) without executing the target GPU workload. We evaluate three models (deepseek-v4-flash, gemini-3.7-flash, and gpt-5.6-sol) across 179 combinations of five kernel families, four NVIDIA and AMD GPUs, four parallel runtimes (CUDA, HIP, OpenMP, and OpenCL), and modified code variants, comparing source-only queries, queries with compiler artifacts, and sandboxed agentic analysis. Compiler artifacts raise the share of arithmetic-intensity estimates within a factor of 1.20 of profiled values from 8-14% to 26-32%, while the resulting bandwidth-bound or compute-bound classifications are correct in 78-92% of runs. With compiler artifacts, FLOP estimates meet the 1.20x tolerance in 59-88% of runs, compared with 36-40% for device-memory traffic. Agentic analysis increases the share of arithmetic-intensity estimates within the 1.20x tolerance by 6.5 percentage points for gpt-5.6-sol, at 3.6x the median cost and 2.5x the median latency of compiler-assisted queries; the other models incur higher costs without a statistically clear improvement. These findings suggest LLM estimates could help prioritize optimization when GPU access is constrained, while identifying cache-dependent device-memory traffic as the main obstacle to precise arithmetic-intensity prediction.

Local Thermodynamic Reconstruction of Impurity Entropy

Hang Su • 2026-11-24

Resolving the thermodynamic entropy associated with a local impurity in a spatially extended interacting quantum bath remains a numerical challenge. We develop a Local-Maxwell approach that reconstructs the impurity entropy from local observables in worldline quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) simulations. By integrating their temperature responses along the impurity coupling, the method avoids separate estimates of extensive entropies. For nonnegative worldline expansions with bounded local couplings, we establish bath-size independent bounds on all factorial moments of the impurity vertex count. For even vertex counts, the Maxwell estimator variance remains bounded as the coupling approaches zero. The transverse-field Ising benchmark reproduces exact free fermion results, while an interacting XXZ chain demonstrates up to several hundredfold improvement in statistical efficiency over conventional extensive thermodynamic integration. In a three-channel XXZ impurity junction, we reveal entropy recovery upon cooling and a nonmonotonic coupling dependence of the finite-temperature entropy contrast. These results establish local thermodynamic reconstruction as an efficient approach to impurity entropy in interacting quantum baths.

Anomalous front propagation in many-body quantum transport with memory-dependent resetting

Gabriele de Mauro, Manas Kulkarni, Satya N. Majumdar • 2026-11-24

We introduce an exactly solvable framework for non-Markovian quantum many-body dynamics based on stochastic resetting with memory. Applicable to arbitrary time-independent Hamiltonians, including interacting systems, our framework provides an exact representation of the dynamics with memory in terms of the corresponding reset-free evolution, which can be accessed either analytically or numerically. We demonstrate the power of this approach by studying domain-wall melting in a one-dimensional chain of noninteracting fermions, obtaining exact analytical results for the density profile, the local structure of the front edge, the extreme-value statistics of the rightmost fermion, and the integrated current. Remarkably, memory transforms ballistic transport into logarithmically slow propagation without arresting spreading. While the macroscopic density profile retains its original scaling function, the front edge broadens as $\sqrt{\ln t}$ rather than $t^{1/3}$ at late times and develops a qualitatively different edge structure. Furthermore, the extreme-value statistics change from Tracy--Widom to Gaussian, while both the mean and variance of the integrated current grow logarithmically in time. Our predictions are in excellent agreement with numerical simulations. These results reveal how memory can fundamentally alter quantum many-body transport and its fluctuations.

Online V-Learning with Adaptive Longitudinal Data

Aniruddhan Ganesaraman, Michael R. Kosorok • 2026-11-24

Reinforcement learning methods for estimating optimal dynamic treatment regimes are increasingly applied in mobile health (mHealth), where treatment decisions are made continually. Luckett et al. (2020) proposed V-learning, which estimates an optimal policy from offline trajectories. In practice, mHealth policies are updated online. Batch updating, in which the policy is re-estimated after each block of new data, is a tractable middle ground between offline learning and updating after every observation. However, because each batch's treatment policy is estimated from data pooled across patients in earlier batches, the resulting data are adaptively collected and patient trajectories are dependent, a setting that offline theory does not address. We develop statistical theory for V-learning in this setting with varying, deterministic batch sizes. For each fixed batch, as the number of patients grows, we establish uniform consistency of the estimated value function, consistency of the estimated policy, a functional central limit theorem over the policy class, asymptotic normality of the estimated value of the learned policy, and a consistent variance estimator. To first order, the online procedure performs as well as an oracle that deploys the optimal policy after the first batch. With the number of patients fixed and the number of batches growing, the estimated value function and policy remain consistent under a persistent identifiability condition. In simulations, as the number of patients grows with the number of batches fixed, confidence intervals attain near-nominal coverage under adaptive assignment and the online procedure closely matches the oracle. With a small cohort followed over many batches, the learned policies improve steadily.

Statistical Process Monitoring of Functional Linear Models

Davide Forcina, Antonio Lepore, Kamran Paynabar • 2026-11-24

Monitoring the stability over time of the relationship between a functional response and multiple functional covariates is important in many industrial applications, either because the relationship itself is the quality characteristic of interest or because it underpins a predictive model whose validity must be verified as the process evolves. Existing methods either assume the model estimated in Phase I to be stable and monitor the resulting residuals, or re-estimate its coefficients at each sampling stage, which requires several observations to be available at a time. In this article, we propose the regression coefficient function (RCF) control chart to monitor functional linear models (FLMs) in which, at each sampling stage, an individual observation of the functional response and an arbitrary number of functional covariates become available. The proposed method employs an exponentially weighted moving loss to sequentially update estimates of the regression coefficient functions and the intercept function, which are monitored through Hotelling's $T^2$ and squared prediction error (SPE) control charts. A post-signal diagnostic procedure is also provided to support the identification of the source of the detected change. A Monte Carlo simulation study demonstrates that the proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art methods, particularly against small and persistent shifts, and a case study on monitoring the relationship between the air/fuel ratio and engine state variables in an automotive after-treatment system illustrates its practical applicability.

Compositional Reachset-Conformant Modeling for Managing Uncertainty in the Development of Cyber-Physical Systems

Laura Lützow, Francesco Pio Urbano, Tobias Düser, Matthias Althoff • 2026-11-24

The development of cyber-physical systems (CPS) proceeds through iterative design revisions, and compliance with safety requirements must be re-established after every revision, typically based on a system model. Conventional models, however, predict only the most likely behavior, leading to model-plant mismatch that often surfaces only during late-stage testing, when redesign is most expensive. This article presents reachset-conformant modeling as a framework for managing uncertainty throughout the development process: instead of a single trajectory, a reachset-conformant model predicts a set of behaviors, calibrated from data to conservatively enclose the true system behavior with a statistical guarantee. Safety properties established on the model therefore transfer to the realized system. Beyond a practical introduction to reachset-conformant identification and its benefits for CPS development, our main theoretical contribution is a closure result: reachset conformance is preserved under serial composition, parallel composition, and feedback interconnection, and hence under any architecture assembled from these primitives. Subsystem models identified independently from local measurements can thus be substituted into the system architecture, and the interconnected model inherits a coverage guarantee from the subsystem guarantees. We complement this result with update strategies that adapt the uncertainty sets after design changes without restarting the identification workflow. Experiments on an automotive brake-system test bench demonstrate that a) a single model covers all operating conditions and hardware variants, b) late-stage modifications are absorbed by uncertainty set updates, c) a black-box hydraulic unit is conservatively abstracted from input-output data alone, and d) independently identified subsystem models form a conformant model by construction.

Dark Matter Assisted Axion-Magnetic Resonance

Chen Sun, Si-Yu Wu, Seokhoon Yun • 2026-11-24

An oscillating axion dark matter (DM) background can enhance the conversion of another relativistic axion into photons when traversing a magnetic field. The DM background rotates photon polarization relative the magnetic orientation, which compensates the photon's and the propagating axion's dispersion relation mismatch. We relate this mechanism to Axion Magnetic Resonance (AMR) and apply it to stellar axions in the starburst galaxy M82. We analyze its enhancement to the resulting X-ray signals while accounting for the finite magnetic domain size, dark matter coherence length, DM density morphology, plasma profiles, and the detector response. We derive strong constraints using existing X-ray data from NuSTAR. We show that this resonant conversion can lead to $\mathcal O(10^2)$ enhancement in the photon-axion conversion probability, improving the upper limits on axion's photon coupling over part of the mass range where ordinary conversion is suppressed. We also show that this type of resonance predicts distinctive features in the hard X-ray spectrum, which is moderately favored by the data over the background only model, yet it is currently still consistent with statistical fluctuations.
