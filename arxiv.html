Magnons in solids with correlated atomic and nanoscale disorder

Alex Moorhouse, Joseph Barker, Michele Simoncelli • 2026-11-25

Atomic defects and disorder affect both the microscopic magnon excitations and the macroscopic physical properties of real-world magnetic materials. The rationalization of this relation has hitherto remained elusive, because of the intertwined challenges of simulating with quantum accuracy atomic- and nanoscale inhomogeneities and correlations, and their effect on the magnon spectral function determining observables such as spin stiffness. Here, we introduce a theoretical and computational framework to address these challenges. First, we develop the Coupled Optimization of SpIN Environment (COSINE) algorithm for efficiently relaxing the atomic structure and magnetic configuration of a solid with defects or disorder to a local energy minimum. Then, we discuss how to efficiently obtain exchange interactions accounting for local compositional, structural, and magnetic-moment inhomogeneities. Finally, we develop a paraunitary unfolding approach for Bogoliubov bosons, which allows us to predict the magnon spectral function in materials with any magnetic order, including systems with canting and full, partial, or no magnetic-moment compensation. We showcase our approach in a representative defective ferrimagnet for generality, and then combine it with state-of-the-art spin-informed machine-learning potentials to quantitatively predict how nonuniformities in the spatial distribution of vacancy defects control blurring of effective magnon bandstructures.

LiveVAE: Causal Window Attention Makes Efficient Streaming Video VAE

Jiachen An, Sijie Zhao, Le Li, Hongyu Liu, Zhiyuan Li, Tianyu Yang, Xiaoyu Kong, Xunliang Cai, Yong Zhang, Chi-Man Pun, Xiaoming Wei, Xiaodong Cun • 2026-11-25

While video diffusion models have grown faster and increasingly streamable, the video VAE remains a primary latency bottleneck across interactive generation and editing pipelines. Existing VAEs and their acceleration methods rely mainly on heavy convolutions or full frame attention, and most focus only on decoding, leaving encoding costly for streaming conditional generation and editing. This raises a natural question: Do video VAE encoding and decoding actually require such global spatiotemporal modeling? By analyzing the effective receptive fields of pretrained video VAEs, we find that both encoding and decoding a patch rely almost entirely on its spatial and temporal neighbors. To exploit this strong locality, we introduce LiveVAE, a streaming encoder and decoder built on causal window attention, which cuts per frame compute while keeping the temporal KV cache bounded for streaming. Decoupled encoder/decoder distillation followed by joint finetuning preserves the pretrained latent space, making LiveVAE a drop-in replacement for the original VAE. On MiniMax-H3, LiveVAE achieves 5.2-6.2x faster encoding and 3.0-3.3x faster decoding than the official VAE with higher PSNR. Paired with our dedicated inference infrastructure, LiveVAE pushes the speedup to 13.4x with almost no quality loss. Code and models will be released.

Schrödinger's Legacy: The Wave Function and Quantum Reality

Davide Romano, Massimiliano Badino • 2026-11-25

The article discusses how Schrödinger arrived at the formulation of wave mechanics and his legacy on the philosophy of quantum mechanics, focusing in particular on the metaphysics of the wave function. Schrödinger's interpretation of the wave function as electron charge density is generally dismissed or presented only as a curious historical example in the literature. On a closer look, however, while this interpretation was eventually abandoned in favor of Born's statistical interpretation, Schrödinger's idea survived in modern realist approaches to the wave function, such as the mass-density approach and the multi-field interpretation. It also proved to be of great utility in modern techniques and approximations in quantum chemistry to compute the shape of atoms and molecules. Overall, Schrödinger's interpretation of the wave function and his realist understanding of quantum mechanics paved the way to modern attempts to define a clear ontology of the microscopic world, a tradition that survived the Copenhagen interpretation of Bohr and Heisenberg and that is still present today.

The Look-Elsewhere Effect in Searches for New Physics: from Classical Methods to Machine Learning

Gaia Grosso, Sergio Rodriguez-Benitez, Louis Lyons • 2026-11-25

The look-elsewhere effect (LEE) arises when the location of an effect in parameter space is not specified in advance: scanning many locations inflates the significance of the largest excess, and the local $p$-value must be corrected into a global one. A well-established framework, rooted in the work of Davies and operationalised for particle physics by Gross and Vitells, provides this correction for scans over continuous parameters. Some aspects, however, remain unsettled. The definition of "elsewhere" is ambiguous and ranges from a single distribution to an entire experimental programme. Moreover, the analytical shortcuts of the classical framework do not carry over to searches based on machine learning (ML), which are affected by an implicit post-selection bias that depends on the model's resolution and expressiveness. This review covers the LEE in searches for new physics at colliders, from its statistical foundations to these modern challenges. We discuss the corrections for continuous scans and for multiple discrete tests, illustrated with simple numerical examples, and the interaction between ML and the LEE, which we connect to the broader literature on selective inference. We highlight open problems, including the computational cost of empirical calibration for complex models and the development of analytical shortcuts for ML test statistics, and offer recommendations for reporting discovery significance in both classical and ML-based searches in particle physics.

Interpretable and Accurate Hybrid Decision Trees with Selective Case-Based Splits

Cristiano Landi, Alessio Cascione, Riccardo Guidotti • 2026-11-25

Decision trees are among the most well-known interpretable models in Machine Learning. Over the years, a wide variety of strategies have been proposed to define split conditions within the greedy learning framework, aiming to guide instance routing and enhance predictive performance. These strategies range from axis-parallel and oblique splits based on feature values to proximity-based splits using exemplar instances and more complex hybrid approaches. However, no single method has emerged as universally superior. In this work, we present two key contributions to the construction of greedy decision trees. First, we propose a hybrid tree architecture that integrates both feature-based and case-based splits, allowing for heterogeneous splits throughout the tree. Second, we introduce a novel type of split that leverages a selective case-based approach: when a split is needed, our method searches for the most informative subset of features to define the split, effectively balancing the specificity of case-based methods with the simplicity of feature-based ones. Experiments on a diverse set of tabular datasets show that our method outperforms standard decision trees and achieves performance on par with ensemble methods, while producing shallow trees that retain or even improve predictive accuracy.

Robust large-scale approximate solutions of the constrained Kemeny aggregation ranking problem

Xuan Kien Phung, Sylvie Hamel • 2026-11-25

The Kemeny aggregation ranking problem consists of computing the consensus rankings of an election with respect to the well-known Kemeny-Young voting method. These consensus rankings satisfy many fundamental properties and are the geometric medians of the votes in the election under the Kendall-tau distance which counts the number of pairwise disagreements. While having various applications in computational social choice, machine learning, operations research, and biology, the problem of computing the consensus rankings is NP-hard and encounters a severe bottleneck in practice. In this paper, we consider the more general Kemeny aggregation ranking problem under immutable input pairwise constraints and propose robust and scalable approximate methods with provable guarantees. We thus provide an algorithmic solution to the more general constrained problem of incorporating a posteriori immutable pairwise constraints into past rankings (e.g., historical voter ballots or legacy laboratory assays) without incurring the cost of generating new data. Our base algorithm $Γ$-XCC provides a source of rankings with a bounded constant approximation factor. $Γ$-XCC generalizes the Copeland ranking method and satisfies the constrained Condorcet consistency property, an extension of the Condorcet winner criterion for elections with immutable pairwise constraints. Thereby, our methods provide large-scale solutions that address the reliability and explainability issues. Our algorithms are also applicable to weighted tournaments with input pairwise constraints and satisfy similar properties in this larger context.

Model Weights, Airlocks and Sandboxes: A Call for Technical Validation of Re-identification Tests under the EDPB Anonymisation Guidelines

Rickard Brännvall, Henrik Forsgren, Erik Ylipää, Aslak Felin, Michael Popoff, Susanne Stenberg • 2026-11-25

The Guidelines 02/2026 on anonymisation of the European Data Protection Board (EDPB), published for consultation in July 2026, assess anonymity relative to the entities that may access data. They bring AI models into scope and call for testing, but do not say how a re-identification test should be designed or validated, or how far its result counts as evidence. Building on our reply to the consultation, we describe a framework based on a secure processing environment whose outputs are reviewed before release (an airlock), and distinguish forms of release and three types of evidence: formal, empirical and indirect. We raise seven open questions, each with a scenario, and examine their technical side in three settings: the release of model weights (the trained parameters of a machine learning model), review at an airlock, and regulatory sandboxes, in which an authority supervises development and testing. In each setting the assessment depends on how far a test result can be relied on, since a failed re-identification attempt may mean only that the test was too weak. Drawing on measurement practice in other regulated fields, we call for re-identification tests to be validated as measurement methods, with stated detection limit, bias and uncertainty.

TIDE-Flow: Fine-Grained Temporal Evidence and Structured Displacement Matching for Spike Optical Flow

Hanwen Liang, Wenxuan Liu, Wenxin Huang, Jingling Yuan, Zhaofei Yu, Xian Zhong, Tiejun Huang • 2026-11-25

Spike cameras capture high-speed motion through an integrate-and-fire mechanism, providing fine-grained temporal observations without exposure blur. However, their binary streams encode accumulated illumination rather than instantaneous intensity, making reliable motion estimation challenging. Existing spike-based methods exploit temporal aggregation to construct motion representations, but this process may weaken fine-grained firing patterns. Meanwhile, correlation-based estimators often rely on learned matching features without explicitly characterizing the spatial distribution and ambiguity of candidate displacements. To address these challenges, we propose TIDE-Flow, a spike-based optical flow estimation framework that jointly exploits Temporal Integration Evidence (TIE) and Displacement Evidence Encoding (DEE). TIE combines raw spike patterns, multi-scale firing statistics, temporal moments, and boundary-aware interval cues, preserving complementary temporal information before spatial feature compression. DEE further integrates structured candidate-grid features with explicit displacement statistics to characterize matching geometry and competing correspondences. Both components are incorporated into a difference-correlation recurrent refinement framework, enabling effective temporal motion modeling and correspondence estimation. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of TIDE-Flow, with controlled ablations validating the contributions of temporal and displacement evidence modeling.

Probing Visual Idiosyncrasies in Text-to-Image Models

Yongqian Peng, Hongjie Li, Yichao Zhou, Chi Zhang, Yixin Zhu • 2026-11-25

Text-to-Image (T2I) models turn short text descriptions into images, and the leading models now produce images of comparable visual quality. Whether their outputs still carry model-specific characteristics matters for attribution, for model comparison beyond aggregate quality, and for evaluations that assume anonymity. We ask whether images from frontier T2I models can still be classified by source, and which visual properties make this possible. Here we show that a standard ResNet-50 identifies the source of an image among HiDream-O1-Dev, FLUX.2-dev, HunyuanImage-3.0, and Qwen-Image-2512, the four highest-ranked open-source models on the Text-to-Image Arena, with 100.00% accuracy on held-out prompts. Human participants reach only 62.61% on the pairwise version of this task, so the distinguishing cues are not visually obvious. They are also not tied to a particular classifier and survive severe image corruptions and out-of-distribution prompts. We trace them to visual idiosyncrasies at three levels. Low-level texture statistics alone let a lightweight multi-layer perceptron match ResNet-50. Object layout, depth, and albedo estimated from the images each carry a source signal. Image captions reveal model-specific preferences in style and background that the prompts never specify. A one-sentence hint about these cues raises human accuracy by 9.03 percentage points. These idiosyncrasies make source classification a tool for measuring similarity between models and between real and generated images, and they caution against assuming anonymity in human preference evaluation of T2I models.

Lattice gauge theory computation of $1/m_Q$ and $1/m_Q^2$ corrections to the quarkonium potential

Michael Eichberg, Marc Wagner • 2026-11-25

We compute the static quark-antiquark potential and its $1/m_Q$ and $1/m_Q^2$ corrections, i.e.\ the $1/m_Q$ potential and all $1/m_Q^2$ potentials, which can be expressed in terms of correlators of two field strength insertions, in SU(3) lattice gauge theory and provide parameterizations for quark-antiquark separations $0.2 \, \text{fm} \lesssim r \lesssim 0.7 \, \text{fm}$. The field strength insertions in generalized Wilson loops are renormalized using gradient flow, which also leads to small statistical uncertainties. All potentials are extrapolated to zero flow time and, in contrast to previous lattice studies, to the continuum, using three lattice spacings and four flow times. The spin dependent potentials are matched to the $\overline{\text{MS}}$ scheme at NLO and provided at $μ= 3 \, \text{GeV}$. They can easily be converted to specific heavy quark masses, e.g.\ for Born-Oppenheimer effective field theory studies of quarkonium.

Routeability Before Routing: Routeability Audit Protocol (RAP) and RouteabilityBench for Audited LLM Model Selection

Yihang Lu, Denica Kjorvezir, Ana Gjorgjevikj, Carola Doerr, Tome Eftimov • 2026-11-25

LLM routing aims to choose, for each prompt, which model in a portfolio is most likely to answer correctly. This only makes sense when the benchmark contains enough prompt-specific variation for different models to be best on different inputs. On coarse-scored LLM benchmarks, however, many models often tie for the best score, and the single best model may already leave little room for improvement. We therefore propose a routeability-first audit, instantiated as RAP and RouteabilityBench, a runnable claim-verification package. The audit is retrospective: it runs once the benchmark-portfolio score matrix exists and before router comparisons are interpreted as general evidence, rather than certifying a portfolio that has not yet been evaluated. Across four benchmarks and 66 LLMs, generic routing enhancements such as larger embedding sets, dimensionality reduction, selective abstention, clustering, and stronger regressors do not consistently outperform the single best model. The clearest positive evidence is concentrated in portfolio-dependent Omni-MATH settings, while GPQA, MMLU-Pro, and IFEval show different ways in which routing can remain hard or predictor-sensitive. Our practical recommendation is that routing gains should be reported together with routeability diagnostics, tied-best analysis, portfolio construction details, and family-corrected statistical tests.

Rugosity as a measurement-induced relative entropy

Lucas Chibebe Céleri, Diogo de Oliveira Soares-Pinto • 2026-11-25

Quantum-state rugosity quantifies the departure of a quantum state from the flat superposition associated with a prescribed basis. We show that rugosity equals the classical relative entropy generated by the binary flat-state measurement. This identity gives two hypothesis-testing interpretations. Rugosity is the asymptotic discrimination exponent under repeated flat-state measurements and the exact finite-copy exponent under unrestricted quantum testing with zero type-I error. For smoothly parametrized states, we relate its local variation to the Fisher information accessible through the prescribed measurement. Using the local expansion of quantum relative entropy, we derive a bound on rugosity sensitivity in terms of the entropy-production susceptibility of a subsequent thermal reset. This susceptibility is given by the Bogoliubov-Kubo-Mori Fisher information. Finally, we formulate a measurement-dependent quantum speed limit whose endpoint distinguishability is determined by rugosity. These results connect quantum-state rugosity with statistical discrimination, thermal reset, and the geometry of experimentally accessible probability distributions.

Comparative Study of Deep Learning Architectures for Brain Tumour Classification

Prisha Gupta, Pradeep Kumar Gupta • 2026-11-25

Accurate and early classification of brain tumours from magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is essential for treatment planning, yet many published deep learning studies are trained and tested on small, single-source datasets, reporting accuracies that may not hold outside these datasets. This study compares six deep learning architectures for four-class classification (healthy, meningioma, glioma and pituitary tumour): a baseline convolutional neural network (CNN), an attention-based CNN, ShuffleNet, and EfficientNet-B0, ResNet-50 and Xception augmented with the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM). The models were trained on a corpus assembled from four publicly available MRI datasets. Because these datasets carry no patient identifiers, a leakage-aware pipeline was developed that removes exact duplicates using MD5 hashing (550 images) and near-duplicates using perceptual hashing (8,024 images), and assigns visually similar images to the same fold during 5-fold group cross-validation. A leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) test on a fully held-out dataset was used to measure cross-dataset generalisation. Under a conventional random split, ShuffleNet achieved 97.1% accuracy with 1.2M parameters. Under leakage-aware group K-fold cross-validation, ShuffleNetV2 (98.41%) and EfficientNet-B0 (98.23%) were statistically indistinguishable (paired t-test, p = 0.19), and both significantly outperformed the custom CNNs. On the held-out dataset, accuracy dropped for every model: EfficientNet-B0 generalised best (78.4%), while ShuffleNetV2 fell to 66.2%. Glioma-meningioma confusion was the dominant error mode, and Grad-CAM maps showed that predictions were driven by tumour regions. These results indicate that in-distribution accuracy alone overstates real-world performance and that leakage-aware and cross-dataset evaluation should be reported alongside it.

We introduce a new Python package \texttt{periapsis}, which computes posterior distributions for a variety of orbital parameters given astrometric and/or radial velocity data. \texttt{periapsis} not only returns posterior distributions, but also a variety of statistics assessing convergence and fit metrics as well as plotting capabilities for visualizing the orbital systems. A general-purpose electron density model for molecular chemistry

Paolo Pegolo, Joseph W. Abbott, Michele Ceriotti • 2026-11-25

Universal machine-learning potentials predict molecular structure and stability at the accuracy of quantum mechanics, for a fraction of the cost. Properties derived instead from knowledge of the electronic structure give profound insights into chemical reactivity, but are not accessible with the same ease or transferability. We train PET-rho-OMol25, a model of the ground-state electron density and spin polarization of molecular systems on four million OMol25 structures in multiple charge and spin states. The model makes predictions for entire proteins in a few seconds on a single GPU, and enables estimation of important diagnostics of molecular structure and reactivity, such as vibrational Stark measurements, halogen $σ$-holes, hydrogen-bond basicities and core-level shifts, as well as spin density for redox chemistry and magnetism. PET-rho-OMol25 has an accuracy close to density-functional theory, with its least reliable predictions flagged by its built-in uncertainty estimator, for most properties and classes of compounds. It can be used as a drop-in replacement for electronic-structure calculations of the density, making insightful analyses available for larger and more complex structures.

Hierarchical Graph Metanetworks

Ioannis Kalogeropoulos, Giorgos Bouritsas, Yannis Panagakis • 2026-11-25

The emerging field of weight space learning has demonstrated the potential to enable metanetworks, i.e. models that process neural network (NN) data, to support several steps of the machine learning lifecycle, from model optimisation to evaluation and adaptation. However, this potential has yet to materialise beyond small-scale MLPs or CNNs: most current models, including message-passing based graph metanetworks (GMNs), operate exclusively at the finest level of granularity, processing every neuron and weight of the input model. Their cost therefore scales with the number of parameters. Yet NNs are inherently organised hierarchically, with neurons forming layers and layers forming blocks and modules. Additionally, several of their properties, such as Lipschitz upper bounds and generalisation measures, are compositions of layer- and block-level quantities. To incorporate this inductive bias, we introduce Hierarchical Graph Metanetworks (H-GMNs), which leverage the input architecture's hierarchy to coarsen and refine the graph using permutation-symmetry-preserving pooling and un-pooling layers. Further, we extend H-GMNs to Transformers by introducing a graph construction that preserves permutation symmetries and enables multi-level pooling. Our evaluation includes new ResNet-18, ViT and varying ViT model zoos of up to 86M parameters, on tasks spanning model evaluation (an analytic Lipschitz bound proxy, generalisation and robustness prediction), testing (backdoor detection) and editing (pruning). Experimental results show that, compared to non-equivariant alternatives and flat GMNs, H-GMNs are markedly more sample-efficient, and their cost remains nearly constant as metanetwork depth grows, allowing deeper and more expressive processing where flat GMNs run out of memory, without compromising accuracy, which in certain cases considerably improves.

Privileged On-Policy Distillation for Calibrated Alzheimer's Diagnosis With Missing Modalities

Vo Minh Khoi Nguyen, Dat Tien Chung, Quang Minh Khoa Le, Duc Khang Nguyen, Le Thien Phuc Nguyen, Hoang Thien Nguyen, Minh Huu Nhat Le, Phat K. Huynh, Cuong Tuan Nguyen, Thanh Huy Nguyen, Ulas Bagci • 2026-11-25

Alzheimer's disease is diagnosed from clinical, biospecimen and imaging evidence, but few patients undergo every test. Because these modalities are complementary, a missing one has many plausible values. Most existing methods leave it out or train on one estimate, so their classifier never sees its other values, and the estimate is never checked against the diagnosis. We address both gaps with on-policy distillation: the model fills in the missing modalities itself, and teachers with information available only in training grade these fills. Our method, Graded Rollout Imputation with Privileged teachers, draws several fills, one modality at a time. An imputation teacher that knows the diagnosis grades each step, bringing the outcome into the imputation as statistical practice recommends. A diagnosis teacher that sees all real modalities supplies a soft target. Trained on fills that vary, the model learns a confidence that reflects what the missing tests leave open, and it diagnoses from a single rollout. On the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative cohort, it outperforms state-of-the-art baselines. On two external cohorts, it remains competitive in discrimination and, even after every method is recalibrated, its calibration error is level with the lowest on both. Added to three of these baselines, the teachers improve each of them. Our method offers a single, well-calibrated model for whatever combination of tests a patient has received.

ENSO-linked redistribution of post-monsoon tropical cyclone energy between the Arabian Sea and the Bay of Bengal: 1982--2025

Prashant Saini, Rishikesh Yadav, Sarita Azad • 2026-11-25

Tropical cyclones in the North Indian Ocean (NIO) form in two sub-basins, the Arabian Sea (AS) and the Bay of Bengal (BoB), which are usually studied separately, with their relationship to the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) treated as stationary. We develop a joint inter-basin statistical framework and apply it to 416 disturbances from quality-controlled IBTrACS best tracks for 1982--2025, with harmonised IMD and JTWC intensities, ERA5 and ERSSTv5. The framework tests whether ENSO changes the basin total or redistributes energy between the sub-basins, separates its effect on formation from that on intensification, attributes each sub-basin's response to environmental terms along individual tracks, and tests stationarity and the record length needed to detect trends. In October-December, ENSO redistributes rather than changes the total: for a one-standard-deviation increase in Niño-3.4 (about 1 $^\circ$C), accumulated cyclone energy (ACE) is multiplied by 1.89 in the AS and by 0.63 in the BoB, while the NIO total shows no detectable change (factor 0.99 [0.74, 1.32]). ENSO acts on intensification, with no detectable effect on formation: the odds that a cyclonic storm becomes very severe rise about fourfold in the AS and halve in the BoB, through lower vertical wind shear (VWS) over the AS and weaker low-level vorticity over the BoB. The relationship has changed: El Niño lowered total NIO energy in 1982--2003, but in 2004-2025 AS gains balanced BoB losses (change p = 0.016). Very severe storms and rapid intensification increased in the AS only, and no 15-year window detects this increase, so about three decades of data are needed. The post-monsoon IOD signal can hardly be separated from ENSO, and August Niño-3.4 gives modest skill for the AS-BoB partition but none for the basin total.

From Shared Patterns to Individual Evidence: Two-Stage Reasoning for Emerging Entities in Temporal Knowledge Graphs

Jinchuan Zhang, Lei Gao, Hongyu Jiang, Chong Mu, Chenang Cai, Ke Yan • 2026-11-25

Temporal knowledge graphs (TKGs) continually introduce emerging entities whose interaction histories evolve from zero-shot debuts into sparse, bursty few-shot trajectories. However, current paradigms are fragmented: pattern-transfer methods lack catalog-wide event memory, whereas online memory models suffer from endpoint-role asymmetry that degrades unseen-answer ranking. Furthermore, sparse early interactions exhibit both long-range dispersion and local redundancy under skewed distribution. To address these challenges, we propose INSPIRE (Integrating Shared Patterns and Individual Evidence for Reasoning), a two-stage forecasting framework over a known catalog. In Stage I, INSPIRE learns an availability-aware shared-pattern predictor that fuses event-updated memory across the full candidate catalog while applying coordinated query- and catalog-side masking. Subsequently, Stage II freezes this predictor and optimizes a lightweight individual-evidence adapter (EA) combining textual semantic priors with correlation-weighted long-range event statistics under support- and role-balanced co-answer supervision with prior-odds debiasing and soft residual alignment. In addition, we decompose evaluation by endpoint roles and track longitudinal emerging-answer cohorts via query-micro and answer-entity macro metrics. Across four benchmarks based on the ICEWS and GDELT event datasets, INSPIRE improves Emerging MRR by 16.73%-88.83% over the strongest baselines. Relative to paired Stage I predictors, EA further increases Emerging MRR by 8.28%-32.63%, Answer-new MRR by 7.81%-41.24%, and cohort macro MRR by 9.64%-39.94%, gains attributed to semantic priors and candidate-level score competition suppressing mismatched established competitors without zero-support prior shortcuts. The official code is publicly available at https://github.com/KcAcoZhang/INSPIRE.

Automatic dual basis sets for self-consistent field calculations

Joonatan Huhtasalo, Susi Lehtola • 2026-11-25

Density functional theory (DFT) has long been the workhorse of electronic structure theory, and it is experiencing a new boom thanks to big data applications and machine learning. A DFT calculation requires the iterative solution of the self-consistent field (SCF) problem, whose cost is the product of (i) the number of SCF steps and (ii) the cost of a single step. The number of SCF steps depends strongly on the initial guess, regardless of the SCF algorithm, and quantitative DFT accuracy is known to require at least a polarized triple-$ζ$ basis set. However, a qualitatively correct solution may often be found already in a smaller \emph{dual basis} (DB), which can then bootstrap a calculation in the full basis set. Traditionally, the DB is chosen by hand for each application to enable robust convergence to the ground state. In this work, we pursue automatic generation of DBs for SCF calculations. Our generator constructs a pruned orbital basis from the target orbital basis for a predefined precision threshold and a given Fock matrix. As expected, the larger the basis set is, the smaller the fraction of kept basis functions. The DBs produced by our algorithm exhibit similar performance for a wide range of systems from the first 4 periods of the periodic table. Ultimately, we find that presently available initial guesses for the Fock matrix are not accurate enough to unlock the use of adaptive basis sets, and believe that the same limitation may hold for similar attempts for basis compactification.
